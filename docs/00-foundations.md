# Mordor — Bialgebraic & Dialgebraic Axioms Framework

**Version 3.1 · The Two Crossing, Closed**

Mordor is a formal system for reasoning about least (μ) and greatest (ν) fixed points of a signature functor, organized as three layers: **bialgebraic axioms** (algebra + coalgebra over one carrier), **dialgebraic axioms** (algebra and coalgebra fused into single maps), and a calculus of **bounds and interaction rules** governing how μ- and ν-axioms mix without collapse.

---
## 1. Ambient Setting

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **M** | The ambient category ("Mordor"); assumed ω-cocomplete, ω-complete, locally cartesian closed |
| **F** : M → M | The signature endofunctor (the Eye): finitary, positive |
| **Ord**, ordinals α, β | Iteration heights for approximation chains |
| ⊥, ⊤ | Initial and terminal object of M (or bottom/top of the lattice of subobjects) |
| μX.F(X) | Least fixed point of F (initial F-algebra carrier) |
| νX.F(X) | Greatest fixed point of F (final F-coalgebra carrier) |

**Postulate M0 (Well-behavedness).** F preserves ω-colimits of chains and ω-limits of chains. This guarantees existence of μ and ν by Lambek-style iteration:

```
μX.F(X) = colim_{α}  F^α(⊥)        (from below)
νX.F(X) = lim_{α}   F^α(⊤)        (from above)
```

**Lambek's Lemma (M0.1).** Both fixed points are exact: F(μF) ≅ μF and F(νF) ≅ νF. No fixed point is merely pre-fixed. This is the "no shortcut through Mordor" clause.

**Convention M0.2 (Internal language & subobject discipline).** Since M is locally cartesian closed, it interprets a dependent internal language. All element-style notation in this framework is internal-language sugar, read categorically as follows:

- `U ⊆ X` denotes a monomorphism `U ↣ X` in M; `U ⊑ V` denotes factorization of `U ↣ X` through `V ↣ X` in the complete lattice **Sub(X)**.
- `x ∈ U` is the internal proposition `U(x)`; no rule ever consumes raw elements — every element-level phrase has a unique categorical reading.
- `F(U) ⊑ V` means the image factorization of `F(U ↣ X)` is contained in V.
- Knaster–Tarski-style bounds (μ4, ν4, Bd2, Bd3) live in the internal subobject lattice of the ambient fixed point (in the fiber over 1 when M is well-pointed).

With this convention, categorical and lattice notation are two renderings of one language and may be interchanged freely.

## 2. Bialgebraic Axioms

An **F-bialgebra** is a triple (X, a, c) with:

```
a : F(X) → X      (the μ-side, an F-algebra)
c : X → F(X)      (the ν-side, an F-coalgebra)
```

### 2.1 μ-Axioms (Inductive Layer)

Let `in : F(μF) → μF` denote the initial algebra.

| # | Axiom | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **μ1** | **Unfold** | `in : F(μF) → μF` is an isomorphism; `in⁻¹` exists. |
| **μ2** | **Fold** | For any F-algebra `a : F(A) → A` there is a unique `fold a : μF → A` with `fold a ∘ in = a ∘ F(fold a)`. |
| **μ3** | **Induction** | If a subobject `U ↣ μF` satisfies `in[F[U]] ⊑ U` — i.e., the image factorization of `in ∘ F(U ↣ μF)` factors through U — then `U ≅ μF`. (No proper sub-bialgebra survives.) |
| **μ4** | **μ-Intro (lattice form)** | In the internal subobject lattice (M0.2): `μF = ⋀ { U ↣ X : F(U) ⊑ U }` — the **least pre-fixed point** of F. |
| **μ5** | **μ-Elim** | If `F(U) ⊑ U` then `μF ⊑ U`. Leastness is a proof rule, not a remark. |

**Derivable:** initiality ⇒ no infinite descending construction chains; μ reasoning is *destruction-friendly, construction-forced*.

### 2.2 ν-Axioms (Coinductive Layer)

Let `out : νF → F(νF)` denote the final coalgebra.

| # | Axiom | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **ν1** | **Unfold** | `out : νF → F(νF)` is an isomorphism. |
| **ν2** | **Ana** | For any F-coalgebra `c : C → F(C)` there is a unique `ana c : C → νF` with `out ∘ ana c = F(ana c) ∘ c`. |
| **ν3** | **Coinduction (Bisim)** | If a relation `R ↣ X × X` is an F-bisimulation — the kernel pair of `c` lifts through F to R, i.e., the standard bisimulation square commutes — then the image of R in `νF × νF` is contained in the diagonal. Bisimilar ⇒ equal. |
| **ν4** | **ν-Intro** | In the internal subobject lattice (M0.2): `νF = ⋁ { U ↣ X : U ⊑ F(U) }` — the **greatest post-fixed point** of F. |
| **ν5** | **ν-Elim** | If `U ⊑ F(U)` then `U ⊑ νF`. Greatestness is a proof rule. |

**Derivable:** coinduction is *construction-friendly, observation-forced*; equality in Mordor is observational equivalence.

### 2.3 Bialgebra Proper

For a bialgebra (X, a, c) both sides coexist on one carrier. The axioms that bind them:

| # | Axiom | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **Harmony** | Precise form: `a ∘ c = id_X` iff a and c are mutually inverse isomorphisms; by Lambek (M0.1) this holds exactly when X is a fixed point of F (`F(X) ≅ X`). Such bialgebras are called *balanced*. No weaker "closeness to identity" is postulated. |
| **B2** | **Distributive law** | Let T be the free monad generated by F (the μ-layer) and D the cofree comonad generated by F (the ν-layer). Interaction is governed by a distributive law of T over the signature functor F: a natural transformation `λ : F∘T ⇒ T∘F` commuting with the monad structure — unit law: `λ_X ∘ F(η_X) = η_{F(X)}`; multiplication law: `λ_X ∘ F(μᵀ_X) = μᵀ_{F(X)} ∘ T(λ_X) ∘ λ_{T(X)}`. Dually, `ρ : D∘F ⇒ F∘D` commutes with the counit and comultiplication of D. All compatibility squares commute. |
| **B3** | **Lifting** | λ lifts T-algebras to T-coalgebras and vice versa: one law rules both sides of the bialgebra. The One Ring binds them. |

## 3. Dialgebraic Axioms

A **(F, G)-dialgebra** on carrier X is a single map serving simultaneously as F-algebra and G-coalgebra:

```
φ : F(X) → G(X)
```

with F, G : M → M. When G = Id these reduce to ordinary algebras; when F = Id they reduce to coalgebras. Dialgebras are the fusion of the μ- and ν-layers.

| # | Axiom | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **Initial dialgebra (μ^FG)** | There is an initial (F, G)-dialgebra `δ₀ : F(μFG) → G(μFG)`. For any dialgebra `φ : F(A) → G(A)` there is a unique `dfold φ : μFG → A` with `G(dfold φ) ∘ δ₀ = φ ∘ F(dfold φ)`. |
| **D2** | **Final dialgebra (ν^FG)** | *Assume the category of (F, G)-dialgebras admits a final object* (not automatic — see §9, OQ2). Then there is a final dialgebra `δ₁ : F(νFG) → G(νFG)`. For any φ there is a unique `dunfold φ : A → νFG` with `δ₁ ∘ F(dunfold φ) = G(dunfold φ) ∘ φ`. |
| **D3** | **Dialgebraic induction** | Any dialgebra invariant closed under δ₀-propagation equals μFG. |
| **D4** | **Dialgebraic coinduction** | Any dialgebraic bisimulation for δ₁ is contained in equality on νFG. |
| **D5** | **Duality** | μ^FG on M = ν^GF on M^op. The dialgebraic layer is self-dual; μ and ν exchange sides when you walk the category backwards. |
| **D6** | **Degeneracy** | μ^{Id,Id} ≅ ⊥ and ν^{Id,Id} ≅ ⊤. The identity functor admits no nontrivial fixed behavior: μX.X = ⊥, νX.X = ⊤. No dialgebra of the Eye sees itself. |

**Note (Hagino extension).** Parameterized dialgebras give types `μα. F(α) → G(α)`, generalizing exponentials, products, and polymorphism in one scheme. Mordor's dialgebra layer is therefore expressive enough to host its own function spaces.

## 4. Bounds

The bound calculus fixes how large μ and ν constructions may grow and how approximation chains converge.

### 4.1 Ordering bounds

| # | Bound | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Bd1** | **Sandwich (μ ≤ ν)** | `μX.F(X) ⊑ νX.F(X)`, with equality iff F is "balanced" (algebraic and coalgebraic semantics agree). |
| **Bd2** | **Knaster–Tarski, lower** | Any **pre-fixed** point X (`F(X) ⊑ X`) bounds μ from above: `μF ⊑ X`. |
| **Bd3** | **Knaster–Tarski, upper** | Any **post-fixed** point X (`X ⊑ F(X)`) bounds ν from below: `X ⊑ νF`. |
| **Bd4** | **Ordinal approximation** | `F^α(⊥) ⊑ μF ⊑ νF ⊑ F^α(⊤)` for every ordinal α; the left chain stabilizes at μ by ω (finitary F), the right chain may need transfinite height — and in fact already does: by Worrell's theorem, for finitary Set functors the final chain stabilizes at ω + ω, not ω (see §9, OQ4). |
| **Bd5** | **Monotone convergence** | For monotone F, both chains are monotone; μ is the first stabilization point of the ascending chain, ν the first stabilization of the descending one. |
| **Bd6** | **Continuity bound** | If F is ω-cocontinuous, μ is reached in ω steps: `μF = ⋁_{n<ω} Fⁿ(⊥)`. If F is only monotone, μ may require arbitrarily large ordinals — the bound is not computable in general. |

### 4.2 Dialgebraic bounds

| # | Bound | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Bd7** | **Dialgebraic sandwich** | `μ^FG ⊑ ν^FG` when the dialgebra order is induced pointwise on Hom(F(−), G(−)). |
| **Bd8** | **Cross bound** | `μ^{F,G} ⊑ ν^{G,F}` and `μ^{G,F} ⊑ ν^{F,G}` under D5-duality: swapping the functors swaps the bound direction. |
| **Bd9** | **Reduction bound** | `μ^{F,Id} = μF` and `ν^{Id,F} = νF`: dialgebraic bounds strictly generalize the bialgebraic ones. |

## 5. Interaction Rules

How μ-axioms, ν-axioms, and dialgebraic axioms interact. These rules are the proof calculus of Mordor.

### 5.1 μ–ν interaction

| # | Rule | Statement | Side condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I1** | **Beck-Chevalley commutation (lower)** | `μX.νY. F(X, Y) ⊑ νY.μX. F(X, Y)` — **Theorem (v1.6):** proof via the stability lemma L2, see §13 | always (least-inside is smaller) |
| **I2** | **Beck-Chevalley commutation (upper)** | `νY.μX. F(X, Y) ⊑ μX.νY. F(X, Y)` — **not a theorem of monotone bifunctors** (finite-chain counterexamples, §11). **Theorem (v1.7, §14):** the rule holds **iff the composite one-step map `a∘b` has a unique fixed point**, iff `μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F` (with `a(x) = νY.F(x,Y)`, `b(y) = μX.F(X,y)`). The counterexamples are exactly the cases where a and b share more than one common fixed point. One-step commutation (I5) is a sufficient route to the collapse | Exact: unique fixed point of `a∘b`; sufficient: derived one-step commutation |
| **I3** | **μ-inside-ν** | `νY. (A + F(Y))` solutions may embed `μX. G(X)` for G ⊑ F componentwise | G sub-functor of F |
| **I4** | **Guardedness** | μ-expressions may occur under ν-constructors only in guarded positions: `μX. G(X)` inside `νY. H(Y)` is legal iff every occurrence of Y guards X (cf. the guarded solution theorem: for iteratable F, guarded equation morphisms have unique solutions) | violation ⇒ divergence of approximation chains |
| **I5** | **μ-ν swap (e-conversion)** | `μX.νY. F ≅ νY.μX. F` holds when F is **dinatural in the fixed-point variables** (Dubuc–Street dinaturality: the canonical mate squares of the μ- and ν-transitions commute) **and the argument graph of those transitions is acyclic** (McCusker–Santamaria: dinaturals compose to dinaturals precisely under acyclicity). The bialgebra is then *balanced* and Bd1 is tight | I1 and I2 both hold: F covariant bifunctor, ω-cocontinuous in Y, dinatural + acyclic in (X, Y) |

### 5.2 Algebra–coalgebra interaction (bialgebra level)

| # | Rule | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **I6** | **fold/ana fusion** | `fold a ∘ ana c` is well-defined when the composite law squares commute; it equals `fold a'` for the fused algebra a' derived via λ (B2). |
| **I7** | **Bisimulation up-to algebra** | A relation R up-to a (i.e., closure under a ∘ F-projection) is contained in equality on νF provided (a, c) is a λ-bialgebra **and** the up-to function is *compatible* with the functor's progression: `R ⟩→ S ⟹ f(R) ⟩→ f(S)` (Pous–Sangiorgi compatibility). **Resolved (v1.4):** in the fibrational setting, λ itself induces the compatible up-to-context function — compatibility in λ-bialgebras is a theorem (Bonchi–Petrișan), so B2's law discharges the side condition. |
| **I8** | **No-confusion** | μ-sorted terms and ν-sorted observations never identify two μ-distinct elements through observation alone, unless forced by I5 balance. |

### 5.3 Dialgebraic interaction

| # | Rule | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **I9** | **D1/D2 pairing** | For a dialgebra φ : F(A) → G(A), `dfold φ` and `dunfold φ` form a conjugate pair: `dunfold φ = (dfold φ^op)⁻¹` in M^op (via D5). |
| **I10** | **μ→ν smuggling** | An inductive proof of `P : μFG → Prop` converts to a coinductive proof of `Q : νGF → Prop` by D5-duality; contrapositive conversion is sound only in the balanced case. |
| **I11** | **Layer collapse guard** | If the distributive law λ of B2 is invertible (a natural isomorphism), the layers are equivalent: `μFG ≅ μF ×_X νG`, where `×_X` denotes the **pullback in M over the common carrier** — the limit of the cospan `μF → X ← νG` whose legs are the comparison maps induced by the λ-bialgebra structure: `fold a : μF → X` on the algebra side, and `cofold c : νG → X` on the coalgebra side (this map exists precisely because λ⁻¹ transports the coalgebra structure c into a G-algebra on X). If either leg fails to exist, `×_X` is undefined and the layers are strictly nested. |

## 6. Consistency & Metatheorems

1. **Theorem (No Catastrophic Collapse).** Mordor is consistent relative to M: every theorem of the framework is an identity of M plus the existence postulates M0. D6 prevents trivial fixed points; I4 prevents unguarded self-reference.
2. **Theorem (Expressive Completeness of Dialgebras).** Products, exponentials, initial algebras, and final coalbras are all (F, G)-dialgebras for suitable F, G. The dialgebraic layer subsumes both bialgebraic layers.
3. **Theorem (Bound Tightness).** Bd1 is tight: there exist F for which μF ⊊ νF strictly (e.g., F(X) = 1 + X on Set: μ is ℕ, ν is ℕ ∪ {∞}).
4. **Theorem (Interaction Soundness).** Every rule I1–I11 preserves the fixed-point semantics; unsound "up-to" reasoning is exactly what fails when I2's side condition fails.

## 7. Reading the Framework

- **μ-axioms** govern *construction*: what can be built, and what proving a universal property costs (induction).
- **ν-axioms** govern *observation*: what can be watched, and when two processes are equal (bisimulation).
- **Dialgebras** fuse the two: a single map that is simultaneously build-rule and watch-rule, with initial/final poles D1/D2.
- **Bounds** are the walls of Mordor: every construction is squeezed between the ascending μ-chain and the descending ν-chain, and Bd1 says the walls never cross.
- **Interaction rules** are the roads between the towers: commutation, fusion, duality, and the guardedness law that keeps the roads from closing into paradox.

*One Ring to bound them all: λ.*
