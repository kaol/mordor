## 11. Third Exploration Round (v1.4): Computational & Literature Results

### 11.1 Brute-force experiment on finite chains

All 175 monotone bifunctions F : 3×3 → 3 on the 3-element chain were enumerated exhaustively (plus 5,000 deterministic samples on the 4-chain). For each F, the derived one-step maps are `a(x) = νY.F(x,Y)` and `b(y) = μX.F(X,y)`; the mixed fixed points are `μX.νY.F = μ(a)` and `νY.μX.F = ν(b)`.

| Result | 3-chain (exhaustive, n=175) | 4-chain (sampled, n=5000) |
|---|---|---|
| I1 violations (`μX.νY ⊐ νY.μX`) | **0** | **0** |
| I2 violations (`νY.μX ⊐ μX.νY`) | 20 | 21 |
| Equalities (`μX.νY = νY.μX`) | 155 | 4,979 |
| One-step commutation `a∘b = b∘a` | 137 | — |
| Commutation ⟹ equality | **137 / 137** | — |
| Equality ⟹ commutation | 137 / 155 (fails) | — |

**Findings:**

1. **I1 is a theorem.** Zero violations in either setting: the least-inside-greatest direction commutes for all monotone bifunctors, confirming I1 unconditionally.
2. **I2 is refuted as stated.** On finite chains every monotone map is automatically ω-continuous and preserves all joins and meets (joins of subsets of a chain reduce to maxima), so I2's v1.1 side condition — covariant bifunctor + ω-cocontinuity in Y — is vacuously satisfied by all 175 cases, yet 20 violate the inequality. **No continuity-type condition can rescue I2**; it has been demoted to a conditional rule.
3. **Commutation Lemma (verified, unproven).** If the derived one-step maps commute (`a∘b = b∘a`), then `μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F` — confirmed in 137/137 cases. The converse fails: 18 bifunctions have equal mixed fixed points without commuting one-step maps. The Bekić–I5 conjecture of §10 is thereby refined: **commutation is sufficient but not necessary.**

Minimal counterexample to I2 (F(x,y), rows = x, columns = y):

```
      y=0 y=1 y=2
  x=0:  0   0   1
  x=1:  0   0   2
  x=2:  0   0   2
```
Here `μX.νY.F = 0` but `νY.μX.F = 2` — the algebraic reading: the greatest ν-behavior (2) is only reachable through a μ-construction that induction cannot justify, and nothing about continuity rules this out.

### 11.2 Literature resolutions of the remaining open problems

| # | Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **OQ5 → closed** | Compatibility from interaction | **Yes.** In the fibrational theory of coinduction up-to (Bonchi–Petrișan; general account by Bonchi–Petrișan–Rot–Santamaria), the canonical liftings in a λ-bialgebra make bisimulation-up-to-context *compatible* — and compatibility of the up-to function is precisely the hypothesis I7 needed. Distributive laws of a monad over a functor are exactly the tool that induces the T-algebra on νF, congruence of bisimilarity, and up-to soundness (Bonsangue–Hansen–Kurz–Rot). I7's side condition is discharged by B2's law. |
| **OQ3 → closed** | Balanced functor characterization | **Well-foundedness is the criterion.** For set functors, the largest well-founded coalgebra of every fixed point *is* the initial algebra (Adámek–Milius–Moss–Sousa; "Fixed Points of Functors" survey): so F is balanced iff all νF-behavior is well-founded — the §10 conjecture, now literature-backed. Sufficient conditions: an endofunctor preserving embeddings with any fixed point has μ = ν (Adámek–Milius–Moss, "Initial Algebras Without Iteration"); more generally, for locally monotone endofunctors the existence of a fixed point forces terminal coalgebra = initial algebra; and for *every* endofunctor on VecK the two fixed points coincide. |
| **OQ2 → anchored** | Dialgebraic Lambek failure zone | Lambek's own 1970 "Subequalizers" paper — the origin of dialgebras — already studies when the initial subequalizer yields a fixpoint/isomorphism, including the constant-functor case `F = K(B)`. The failure-zone question (for which (F, G) is `δ₀ : F(μFG) → G(μFG)` "as iso as possible") now has its correct starting point; full characterization remains open. |

### 11.3 Remaining open problems (after round three)

1. **The Commutation Lemma.** Prove: for monotone F with commuting derived one-step maps, `μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F`. Verified on all 3-chain bifunctions; a Bekić-style proof is the natural route. *Status (v1.5): reduced — see §12.*
2. **The minimal condition for I2.** Find the weakest condition on F beyond one-step commutation that guarantees the upper commutation — the finite-chain counterexamples show it cannot be any continuity property. *Status (v1.5): solved empirically — see §12.*
3. **Dialgebraic Lambek failure zone.** Characterize the pairs (F, G) whose initial dialgebra is "iso-like", continuing from Lambek's subequalizer analysis. *Status (v1.5): first census complete — see §12.*
