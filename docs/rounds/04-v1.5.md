## 12. Fourth Exploration Round (v1.5): The Census Results

### 12.1 The derived-map structure of μν alternation (3-chain census, exhaustive)

Mining identities across all 175 monotone bifunctions F, with derived one-step maps `a(x) = νY.F(x,Y)` and `b(y) = μX.F(X,y)`:

**Unconditional lemmas (held in 175/175 cases; L1, L2 proven in v1.6, see §13):**

| # | Lemma | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | ν-stability of the μ-step fixed point | `a(ν(b)) = ν(b)` — the greatest b-fixed point is a-fixed. | **Proven** (§13) |
| **L2** | μ-stability of the ν-step fixed point | `b(μ(a)) = μ(a)` — the least a-fixed point is b-fixed. | **Proven** (§13) |
| **L3** | Composite collapse, lower | `μ(a∘b) = μ(a) = μX.νY.F`. | **Proven** (§14) |
| **L4** | Composite collapse, upper | `ν(a∘b) = ν(b) = νY.μX.F`. | **Proven** (§14) |

**Exact characterizations (verified equivalent to `μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F` in all 175 cases):**

```
μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F   ⟺   b(μ(a)) = ν(b)   ⟺   a(ν(b)) = μ(a)   ⟺   μ(a∘b) = ν(a∘b)
```

**Consequence — I2 is solved (modulo proofs of L3/L4):** the upper commutation `νY.μX ⊑ μX.νY` holds exactly when the *composite one-step map* `a∘b` has a **unique fixed point**. This is the minimal condition sought: it is a fixed-point-cardinality property of the one-step composite, not a continuity property (consistent with §11: continuity is vacuous on finite chains).

**The Commutation Lemma, sharpened:** sufficiency of one-step commutation (`a∘b = b∘a ⟹ μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F`, 137/137) now reduces to showing that for commuting derived maps, the composite `a∘b` has a unique fixed point. What remains: commutation forces `μ(a∘b) = μ(a)∨μ(b)` and `ν(a∘b) = ν(a)∧ν(b)` (classical pairwise-commutation identities); the gap is proving that derived pairs cannot split these two composites apart. Abstract commuting pairs *can* split them (e.g., a = b = id gives μ(a∘b) = ⊥ ≠ ν(a∘b) = ⊤), so the derived structure (`a(x) = F(x, a(x))`, `b(y) = F(b(y), y)`) must be the decisive ingredient.

### 12.2 Dialgebraic Lambek census on finite poset categories

In a poset-category, a (F, G)-dialgebra is an object X with `F(X) ⊑ G(X)`; the initial dialgebra is the least such X, and `δ₀ : F(A₀) → G(A₀)` is iso iff `F(A₀) = G(A₀)`. Complete census over all endofunctor pairs:

| Category | Endofunctors | Pairs | Initial exists | δ₀ iso rate | Final exists | δ₁ iso rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chain 3 | 10 | 100 | 90 | **66.7%** | 90 | 66.7% |
| Chain 4 | 35 | 1,225 | 1,120 | **62.5%** | 1,120 | 62.5% |
| Diamond M3 | 178 | 31,684 | 31,062 | **82.8%** | 31,062 | 82.8% |

**Findings:**

1. **Classical Lambek is the (F, Id) boundary slice — and it survives perfectly.** In all three categories, 100% of (F, Id) pairs have `δ₀` an isomorphism: initial dialgebras over the identity degenerate to initial algebras and recover Lambek's lemma exactly. The failure zone is entirely interior.
2. **The failure zone is real and substantial:** roughly one third of functor pairs on chains, one sixth on the diamond, have a non-iso `δ₀`. Dialgebraic Lambek is *not* a theorem — it is a property of matched pairs.
3. **Perfect duality symmetry:** initial and final dialgebra existence coincide (whenever one exists, so does the other — 90/90, 1,120/1,120, 31,062/31,062), and the iso rates are identical — direct computational evidence for D5 (the dialgebraic layer is self-dual).
4. **Existence is not guaranteed:** ~10% of chain pairs admit no dialgebra at all (no X with F(X) ⊑ G(X)), confirming v1.2's amendment to D2.

### 12.3 Remaining open problems (after round four)

1. **Prove L3/L4** (`μ(a∘b) = μX.νY.F`, `ν(a∘b) = νY.μX.F`). With these, I2's characterization and the Commutation Lemma both close.
2. **The commuting-composite gap:** show derived commuting pairs cannot have `μ(a∘b) ≠ ν(a∘b)`.
3. **Failure-zone structure beyond posets:** the census covers poset categories; extend to Set-like M and find invariants that predict iso-ness of `δ₀` (candidates: preservation properties of F, G; M3's higher iso rate suggests branching structure helps).
