## 16. Eighth Exploration Round (v1.9): The General Lemma Refuted — Finiteness Is Essential

### 16.1 Refutation: a commuting polar configuration on the real interval

**Counterexample.** Let L = [0,1] (a complete lattice) and `F(x,y) = (x+y)/2`, monotone in each argument. For fixed x the map `v_x(y) = (x+y)/2` has the unique fixed point y = x, so `a(x) = νY.F(x,Y) = x`; dually `b(y) = μX.F(X,y) = y`. Hence **a = b = id**: the derived maps commute trivially, and *every* point is a common fixed point — in particular ⊥ = 0 and ⊤ = 1. Yet `m = μ(a) = 0 ≠ 1 = ν(b) = n`.

So the Commutation Lemma — and with it rule I2 under the commutation side condition — **fails on general complete lattices**. All previously *proven* results survive, since none used commutation: for this F, L1 (a(1) = 1), L2 (b(0) = 0), L3 (μ(a∘b) = μ(id) = 0 = m), L4 (ν(a∘b) = 1 = n), I1 (0 ⊑ 1), and fusion (Fix(a∘b) = Fix(a)∩Fix(b) = [0,1]) all hold, and the I2 characterization of §14.1 correctly predicts the failure — `a∘b = id` has a continuum of fixed points, not a unique one. What the counterexample refutes is precisely the conjecture that commutation forces uniqueness.

**Where the finite machinery breaks.** The band lemmas of §16.2 rest on orbit termination: the `v_⊥`-orbit `⊤ → F(⊥,⊤) → F(⊥,F(⊥,⊤)) → ⋯` must *reach* ⊥. Here it is `1 → 1/2 → 1/4 → ⋯`, an infinite descent that never lands. Accordingly `B = {y : F(⊥,y) = ⊥} = {0}` is trivial, the descent-based proofs cannot start, and the lemma fails. Note also that boundary repulsion (§15.1) is *necessary but not sufficient*: this F satisfies `F(0,y) = y/2 ⊏ y` and `F(x,1) = (x+1)/2 ≻ x` everywhere.

### 16.2 New general lemmas for commuting polar configurations

1. **Corner Lemma.** In any polar configuration, `⊥ ⊏ F(⊥,⊤) ⊏ ⊤`. *Proof:* `F(⊥,⊤) = ⊤` would make ⊤ a fixed point of `v_⊥`, forcing `a(⊥) = ⊤ ≠ ⊥`; `F(⊥,⊤) = ⊥` makes ⊥ fixed for `u_⊤`, forcing `b(⊤) = ⊥ ≠ ⊤`. ∎ Corollary: no polar configuration on any 2-element lattice.
2. **No-Room Lemma.** Let `c = F(⊥,⊤)`. If both open intervals `(⊥,c)` and `(c,⊤)` are empty, then `F(⊥,c) = ⊥` and `F(c,⊤) = ⊤`, so `b(c) = ⊥` and `a(c) = ⊤`; commutation at c gives `a(⊥) = b(⊤)`, i.e. ⊥ = ⊤. ∎ Corollary: **no commuting polar configuration on any complete lattice of height ≤ 3** — in particular none on `M_κ` (a diamond with κ atoms, *any* κ including infinite: c must be an atom-coatom mid, and both rooms are empty).
3. **Band structure (finite lattices).** `B = b⁻¹(⊥) = {y : F(⊥,y) = ⊥}` is a downset and `T = a⁻¹(⊤) = {x : F(x,⊤) = ⊤}` an upset. On a finite lattice both are nontrivial: the `v_⊥`-orbit from ⊤ strictly descends (repulsion) and must terminate at a fixed point of `v_⊥`, which can only be ⊥ — the last step starts above ⊥, so B ∖ {⊥} ≠ ∅; dually T ∖ {⊤} ≠ ∅. Commutation forces **`a(B) ⊆ B`**, **`b(T) ⊆ T`**, and **`B ∩ T = ∅`** (x in the intersection gives `a(b(x)) = a(⊥) = ⊥ = b(⊤) = b(a(x))`). On a finite chain: `B = ↓β`, `T = ↑τ` with `⊥ ⊏ β ⊏ τ ⊏ ⊤`.
4. **Seam lemmas (chains).** From the invariances: **Row-B repulsion** — `x ∈ B, y ≻ β ⟹ F(x,y) ⊏ y` (else `y ⊑ ν(v_x) = a(x) ∈ B`); **Column-T repulsion** — `y ∈ T, x ⊏ τ ⟹ F(x,y) ≻ x` (else x is a prefixpoint of `u_y`, so `b(y) ⊑ x` with `b(y) ∈ T`). Hence the seam cell satisfies **`β ⊏ F(β,τ) ⊏ τ`**. Any commuting polar configuration on a finite chain therefore needs `⊥ ⊏ β ⊏ F(β,τ) ⊏ τ ⊏ ⊤`: **at least five elements**.

### 16.3 Base cases: no commuting polar configuration on chains with ≤ 6 elements

- **3-chain.** The corner value is forced to the middle element c; repulsion plus no room forces `F(⊥,c) = ⊥` and `F(c,⊤) = ⊤`, so `b(c) = ⊥` and `a(c) = ⊤`; commutation at c gives ⊥ = ⊤. ∎
- **4-chain.** The bands are forced to B = {0,1}, T = {2,3}. Then `F(0,2) = 1` (2 ∉ B + repulsion), `F(1,3) = 2` (top repulsion + a-invariance excludes 3), and `F(1,2) = 0` (a-invariance excludes 2, b-invariance excludes 1, row monotonicity bounds by 2) — but column monotonicity demands `F(0,2) ⊑ F(1,2)`, i.e. 1 ⊑ 0. ∎
- **5-chain.** The five points ⊥, β, F(β,τ), τ, ⊤ are distinct, hence β = 1, τ = 3, B = {0,1}, T = {3,4}. Tightness at the top (`F(2,4) = 3` since 2 ∉ T, then `F(2,3) = 3` by column-T repulsion) forces `a(2) = 3 ∈ T`; commutation at 2 then forces `b(2) ∉ B`, i.e. `F(1,2) ≠ 1`, while `F(0,2) = 1` — and row-B repulsion pins `F(1,2) = 0`. Column monotonicity again: 1 ⊑ 0. ∎
- **6-chain.** Three band configurations are possible: (β,τ) ∈ {(1,3), (1,4), (2,4)}. Each dies by the same two patterns, using the top-corner tightness `F(x,N) ∈ (x,N) ∖ {N}` for x ∉ T: (1,3) and (2,4) fall to the column-(β+1) seam `F(0,β+1) ⊑ F(1,β+1) ⊑ F(β,β+1)` with the values forced to `β ∘ 0`; (1,4) branches on `a(2) ∈ {3,4}` and dies either through `a(2) ∈ T` (seam) or through `b(3) = 2` forced by `F(2,3) = 2` and contradicting `b(a(3)) = b(4) ∈ T`. ∎ (case analysis; independently confirmed at sampling level, §16.4)

**Cumulative status:** the finite Commutation Lemma is proven for chains of length ≤ 6 and all complete lattices of height ≤ 3, and conjectured for all finite lattices. The general (infinite) version is *false* (§16.1).

Two structural findings:

1. **The band conditions are the frontier.** Among 2,738 sampled polar configurations (and 7,300 exhaustively reduced violators of chains 3–4), *none* satisfies the band conditions `B∩T = ∅ ∧ a(B) ⊆ B ∧ b(T) ⊆ T`. On chains, commutation fails already at the band level: the violator anatomy gives chain 3: 20/20 instant-kill (B∩T ≠ ∅); chain 4: 6,020 instant-kill, 630 a-invariance failure, 630 b-invariance failure, and **zero** cases where only the middle-band equations fail (perfect 630/630 duality symmetry). A proof that no polar configuration on a finite chain satisfies the band conditions would close the finite Commutation Lemma for all finite chains at once. **(Correction, v2.1 — §18): the premise of this route is false.** Band-satisfying polar configurations exist — exactly 90,720 on the 5-chain alone — so the bands are necessary but not self-contradictory; the obstruction lies deeper, in commutation's propagation consequences (§18.5). The sampling zeros above were sampler artifacts (biased LCG), not evidence.
2. **Census sizes identified.** Monotone bifunctions on the n-chain are exactly plane partitions in the n×n×(n−1) box (MacMahon's product formula): 175 (n=3), 24,696 (n=4), 16.8M (n=5), 5.5×10¹⁰ (n=6) — which is why exhaustive census stops at n = 4 and sampling takes over. ### 16.5 Remaining open problems (after round eight)

1. **Finite Commutation Lemma (sharpened form of the polar gap):** on a finite complete lattice, a commuting derived pair has a unique common fixed point. Proven: chains ≤ 6, lattices of height ≤ 3. Verified: exhaustively on chains 3–4, B2, M3; ~1.2M samples across chains 5–8 and N5. The band conditions are the candidate sufficient obstruction — no sampled or enumerated polar configuration satisfies them. *Advanced (v2.0, §17): the Staircase Squeeze Theorem proves the band conditions self-reinforcing — they automatically pin the derived maps into contradictory geometry — and the frontier is now the single statement that no finite-chain polar configuration satisfies them.*
2. **The infinite landscape:** the general lemma is refuted ([0,1], §16.1). Where is the exact boundary? Open: does the lemma hold under chain conditions — e.g. on ω+1 (DCC but not ACC)? The [0,1] counterexample has neither, and the finite proofs use orbit termination in both directions. Does every counterexample require interpolation room (an element strictly between ⊥ and F(⊥,⊤) and between F(⊥,⊤) and ⊤), as the No-Room Lemma suggests?
