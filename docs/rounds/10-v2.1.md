## 18. Tenth Exploration Round (v2.1): The Bands Are Not Enough

### 18.1 The Staircase Normal Form

**Theorem (Normal Form).** A monotone bifunction F on the finite chain 0 ⊑ … ⊑ N is a polar configuration satisfying both band conditions **iff** its matrix satisfies the cellwise staircase conditions:

1. `F(0,0) = 0` and `F(N,N) = N`;
2. `F(0,y) ⊑ y−1` for all y ≥ 1, and `F(x,N) ⊒ x+1` for all x ⊑ N−1;
3. with `β = max{y : F(0,y) = 0}` and `τ = min{x : F(x,N) = N}`: `F(x,y) ⊑ y−1` whenever x ⊑ β ⊏ y, and `F(x,y) ⊒ x+1` whenever x ⊏ τ ⊑ y.

*Proof.* Polar: `b(0) = 0 ⟺ F(0,0) = 0`; `a(N) = N ⟺ F(N,N) = N`; `a(0) = 0 ⟺` row 0 has no fixed point ≥ 1 `⟺ F(0,y) ⊑ y−1` for y ≥ 1 (if `F(0,y) ⊒ y`, iteration of row 0 from y ascends monotonically to a fixed point ⊒ y — the Abian–Brown-style orbit argument); dually `b(N) = N ⟺ F(x,N) ⊒ x+1`. Band 1: `a(x) ⊑ β ⟺` row x has no fixed point ⊐ β `⟺ F(x,y) ⊑ y−1` for all y ⊐ β (same staircase). Band 2 dual. ∎

The whole polar+band theory is thereby a **finite cellwise constraint problem** — no fixed-point computation needed anywhere. Cellwise corollaries, each a two-line staircase argument requiring no commutation: `β ≥ 1` (β = 0 forces `F(0,1) = 0`, putting 1 ∈ B); `τ ⊑ N−1` (τ = N forces `F(N−1,N) ⊒ N`); `β ⊏ τ` (β ⊒ τ gives `F(β,N) = N` while band 1 caps it at N−1; β = N dies by `F(0,N) = 0` against `F(0,N) ⊒ 1`); `τ ⊒ β+2` (seam: band 2 lifts `F(β,τ) ⊒ β+1 = τ`, band 1 caps it `⊑ τ−1 = β`); and the squeeze pins `b(β+1) ∈ B`, `a(τ−1) ∈ T` come for free.

**Validation.** Chains 3–4 exhaustive (175 / 24,696 matrices): normal form ⟺ semantic polar+bands with **zero** mismatches, and the semantic polar counts (20 / 6,440) match §11–§15 exactly. Zero normal-form failures across all 90,720 exact chain-5 solutions and ~230,000 direct samples on chains 6–12.

### 18.2 The unconditional Middle Dichotomy (V1)

**Lemma (V1).** In any band-satisfying polar configuration, no `i ∈ M = (β,τ)` satisfies `a(i) ⊑ β` and `b(i) ⊒ τ` simultaneously.

*Proof.* `a(i) ⊑ β` gives the row-i staircase `F(i,y) ⊏ y` for y ⊐ β, so `F(i,i) ⊏ i`. `b(i) ⊒ τ` gives the column-i staircase `F(x,i) ⊐ x` for x ⊏ τ, so `F(i,i) ⊐ i`. Contradiction at the diagonal cell. ∎

Unlike the §17.2 dichotomy (V2: no i with `a(i) ⊒ τ` and `b(i) ⊑ β`), V1 needs **no commutation**. Machine-checked: zero V1 violations in all 90,720 exact chain-5 solutions and ~230,000 direct samples on chains 6–12.

### 18.3 Refutation: band-satisfying polar configurations exist — and are numerous

The conjecture of §16.4/§17.5 is **false**. Minimal example, on the 5-chain (chains 3–4 have none, exhaustively):

```
      y=0 y=1 y=2 y=3 y=4
x=0:   0   0   1   1   1
x=1:   0   1   1   2   2
x=2:   0   1   2   3   3
x=3:   0   1   2   3   4
x=4:   0   1   2   3   4
```

β = 1, τ = 3, B = {0,1}, T = {3,4}; derived maps `a = [0,1,3,4,4]`, `b = [0,0,1,3,4]`: polar (a(0)=b(0)=0, a(4)=b(4)=4), both bands hold (`a(B) = {0,1} ⊆ B`, `b(T) = {3,4} ⊆ T`), `m = 0 ≠ 4 = n`, and every proven theorem of §15–§17 holds (pinning, seam, corner, V1). But commutation fails (`a(b(2)) = 1 ≠ 3 = b(a(2))`) and V2 fails at i = 2 — **necessarily**: the normal form forces `F(2,3) = 3`, hence `a(2) = 3 ∈ T` against the pinned `b(2) ⊑ β`.

**Exact census (chain 5):** precisely **90,720** band-satisfying polar configurations (complete DFS over the normal form, 0 semantic failures) — about 0.5% of all 16.8M monotone bifunctions — of which **zero commute** and **zero are V2-clean**. A 9-element companion (β = 2, τ = 5, `a = [0,0,0,4,5,8,8,8,8]`, `b = [0,0,0,1,1,6,7,8,8]`) shows the same band-clean, commutation-dirty anatomy at larger scale.

**Methodological correction.** The round-8/9 uniform-sampling zeros ("no polar configuration with bands OK" on chains 5–12) were sampler artifacts, not evidence: the sequential-fill LCG is biased against polar and band configurations (chain 5: it observed a 0.47% polar rate — below the 0.54% band-satisfying lower bound alone — and zero band-satisfying hits where 90,720 exist). Direct normal-form generation (round-robin over the forced (β,τ) ranges, random cellwise completion) replaces it.

### 18.4 The census at scale: bands + V2 is also not enough

Direct normal-form sampling (round-robin over all forced (β,τ) pairs), all runs with zero V1 violations and zero normal-form failures:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (exact DFS) | 90,720 | **0** (forced by §18.3) | 0 | 1/1 |
| 6 | 40,000 | 4,311 (10.8%) | 0 | 3/3 |
| 7 | 40,000 | 6,417 (16.0%) | 0 | 6/6 |
| 9 | 25,000 | 4,425 (17.7%) | 0 | 15/15 |
| 12 | 15,000 | 2,121 (14.1%) | 0 | 36/36 |

An earlier probe with random (β,τ) draws found the same on chains 8 (72 V2-clean) and 10 (358 V2-clean), likewise with zero commuting.

**Findings.**

1. **The census is a complete dragnet for Commutation Lemma counterexamples.** Commuting polar configurations must satisfy the bands (§16.2(3)), so every band-satisfying polar configuration is a candidate: **~320,000 candidates examined across chains 5–12 — 90,720 of them exhaustively on chain 5 — and zero commute.** The finite Commutation Lemma's verified base is thereby extended from chains ⊑ 6 (hand proofs, §16.3, now independently cross-validated) to strong chain-12 evidence by a method with no sampler bias in the target class. Consistent with the classical theory: common fixed points of commuting monotone operators are governed by constructive Tarski/Abian–Brown orbit arguments (Cousot–Cousot 1979; Abian–Brown and its chain-complete extensions), and the fixed-point structure of monotone self-maps on finite ordered sets is far subtler than continuity conditions suggest.
2. **V2 is also only necessary.** V2-clean band-satisfying configurations exist from chain 6 up (10–18% of the normal-form family). Commutation fails in them one step deeper: every recorded V2-clean failure signature violates the **band-propagation consequences** of commutation — `a(i) ∈ T ⟹ a(b(i)) = b(a(i)) ∈ T` and `b(i) ∈ B ⟹ b(a(i)) = a(b(i)) ∈ B` (from `b(T) ⊆ T`, `a(B) ⊆ B` plus commutation). V2 is exactly the one-step shadow of propagation (`a(i) ∈ T ∧ b(i) ∈ B` would force `a(b(i)) ∈ T ∩ B = ∅`). Observed V2-clean counterexamples die via `a(i) ∈ T` with `a(b(i)) ∈ B` (chains 7, 9, 12 signatures) or `b(a(i)) ∈ T` with `a(b(i)) ∈ B` (chain 6 signature).

### 18.5 Remaining open problems (after round ten)

1. **The propagation frontier.** Under bands + commutation, band membership propagates along the a/b-orbits of every middle index: `a(i) ∈ T ⟹ a(b(i)) ∈ T`; `b(i) ∈ B ⟹ b(a(i)) ∈ B`; and V2 is the one-step shadow. Pinning seeds two orbits into M from opposite bands (`a(τ−1) ∈ T`, `b(β+1) ∈ B`), and each propagation step pulls the next orbit member toward its band. **Conjecture:** bands + V2 + propagation is contradictory on every finite chain — since commutation implies all three, this would prove the finite Commutation Lemma. Next-round probe: add the propagation conditions to the direct sampler and count survivors; a proof would show the two seeded orbit-trees must collide across `B ∩ T = ∅`. **Resolved negatively (v2.2, §19):** bands + V2 + propagation is satisfiable, and so is the stronger rung bands + V2 + P + E (chains 7–12); single-defect configurations survive every rung below full commutation on M. The frontier is now the **corridor value system** `a(b(i)) = b(a(i))` on M (§19.6).
2. **The infinite landscape** (unchanged, §16.5 item 2): ω+1 / DCC boundary; interpolation-room conjecture.
3. **Failure zone** (unchanged, §15.3–15.4).
