## 19. Eleventh Exploration Round (v2.2): The Commutation Ladder and the Single Defect

The propagation frontier of §18.5 was tested directly and it fell: bands + V2 + propagation is satisfiable, and so is every intermediate rung short of commutation itself. This round maps the full ladder between the bands and the Commutation Lemma, shows that the commutation question concentrates entirely on the middle band M — "the corridor" — and finds that exactly one defect always survives, with a universal signature.

### 19.1 The Band Automaticity Theorem

**Theorem.** In a band-satisfying polar configuration, `b ≡ 0` on `B` and `a ≡ N` on `T`. Consequently commutation is automatic on `B ∪ T`, and

> **commutation ⟺ `a(b(i)) = b(a(i))` for all `i ∈ M`.**

*Proof.* For `y ∈ B`: `F(0,y) = 0`, so 0 is a fixed point of column y, and the least fixed point is `b(y) = 0`. For `x ∈ T`: `F(x,N) = N` makes N a fixed point of row x, and N is trivially the greatest, so `a(x) = N`. For `i ∈ B`: `b(i) = 0`, and 0 is the *only* fixed point of row 0 (normal form: `F(0,y) ⊑ y−1` for y ≥ 1), so `a(b(i)) = a(0) = 0`; band 1 gives `a(i) ∈ B`, so `b(a(i)) = 0`. Both sides 0. For `i ∈ T`: band 2 gives `b(i) ∈ T`, so `a(b(i)) = N`; `a(i) = N`, and N is the only fixed point of column N (normal form: `F(x,N) ⊒ x+1` for x ⊑ N−1), so `b(a(i)) = b(N) = N`. Both sides N. ∎

**Validation.** Defect set `D = {i : a(b(i)) ≠ b(a(i))}`: **dOutsideM = 0** across the complete chain-5 census (90,720 configurations) and ~165,000 direct samples on chains 6–12. No commutation defect was ever observed outside the corridor.

### 19.2 The Diagonal Lemma

**Lemma.** For every index z: `b(z) > z ⟹ a(z) > z`, and dually `a(z) < z ⟹ b(z) < z`.

*Proof.* Suppose `b(z) > z`. Column z has no fixed point ⊑ z; pigeonhole on the nondecreasing column map `g(x) = F(x,z)` (`g(x) ≠ x`, `g(0) ⊒ 0`) forces `g(x) ⊒ x+1` for all `x ⊑ z`, so `d = F(z,z) ⊒ z+1`. Iterate row z from z: `y₀ = z`, `y₁ = d ⊒ z+1`, `y_{k+1} = F(z, y_k) ⊒ y_k` by monotonicity. On a finite chain the ascent stabilizes at a fixed point `y*` of row z with `y* ⊒ d > z`. Hence the greatest fixed point satisfies `a(z) ⊒ y* > z`. The second clause is dual. ∎

Machine-checked: **diagViol = 0** on 48,000 direct samples (chains 6, 7, 9, 12). The lemma couples the signs of the μ- and ν-derived maps along the diagonal — a μ-push above forces a ν-push above — an asymmetry in the same direction as I1 (`m ⊑ n`).

### 19.3 The commutation ladder

The rungs between the bands (L0) and commutation (L3), each strictly weaker than the next:

- **L1 (V2):** no `i ∈ M` with `a(i) ∈ T` and `b(i) ∈ B` (§17.2; V1 of §18.2 is unconditional).
- **L2 (P, propagation):** `a(i) ∈ T ⟹ a(b(i)) ∈ T` and `b(i) ∈ B ⟹ b(a(i)) ∈ B`.
- **L2.5 (E, extremes):** `a(i) ∈ B ⟹ a(b(i)) = 0` and `b(i) ∈ T ⟹ b(a(i)) = N`.
- **L3 (commutation on M):** `a(b(i)) = b(a(i))` for all `i ∈ M`.

**Trivial Defect Lemma.** Any `i ∈ M` with profile `(a(i), b(i)) = (T, B)` is automatically a defect: `b(i) ∈ B` gives `a(b(i)) ∈ B ⊑ β`, while `a(i) ∈ T` gives `b(a(i)) ∈ T ⊒ τ`, and `β ⊏ τ`. V2 is therefore *forced* on the corridor for commutation — the §17.2 dichotomy sharpened from "possible" to "certain."

### 19.4 Chain-5 exact anatomy: the corridor is never V2-clean

Exhaustive over all 90,720 band-satisfying polar configurations on chain 5: **|D| = 1 always**, and the unique defect always sits at **i = β+1 = 2** with profile **(T, B)** — the trivial defect, forced by the seam: the normal form pins `F(2,3) = 3` (hence `a(2) = 3 ∈ T`) against the pinned `b(2) ⊑ β`. So `eClean = 90,720` (E is vacuous when the defect is seam-forced), `pClean = peClean = 0`, `v2clean = 0`. Chain 5 dies at L1 for a fully understood structural reason.

### 19.5 Single-defect persistence and the universal L-straddle signature

On chains 6–12 the ladder never closes: **min |D| = 1 at every rung** (`minD = minDv2 = minDpe = 1`) — configurations exist that commute at every index except exactly one, even under bands + V2 + P + E. Single-defect counts (final focused run): chain 6: 9,952 / 12,000; chain 7: 10,160 / 15,000; chain 9: 5,665 / 12,000; chain 12: 2,829 / 9,000.

**The L-straddle signature.** In **100% of single defects**, with `φ = a∘b` (the μν-composite) and `ψ = b∘a` (the νμ-composite):

> `φ(i) < i < ψ(i)`

— the μν-composite pulls the index **down**, the νμ-composite pushes it **up**. The mirrored straddle `ψ(i) < i < φ(i)` ("H") never occurs, in single- or multi-defect configurations; multi-defect signatures are L-dominant with same-side residuals (chain 12: L 17,653, both-below 3,243, both-above 2,839), and no defect with a composite landing exactly on i ("O") was ever observed. The asymmetry points the same way as I1 (`m ⊑ n`): induction-side composites fall, coinduction-side composites rise.

Witness (chain 7, PE-clean, single defect): `β = 1`, `τ = 5`, `D = [3]`, profile `3:MM`, `a = [0,1,2,4,5,6,6]`, `b = [0,0,1,2,6,6,6]`, with `φ(3) = a(2) = 2 < 3 < 6 = b(4) = ψ(3)`; matrix rows `0012233 / 1112333 / 2222334 / 2224444 / 3344555 / 5555666 / 5566666`. Every theorem of §15–§19 holds; only the corridor fails, at one index.

**Consequence.** No condition strictly weaker than L3 — no band-level, profile-level, propagation-level, or extremal-level rung of the ladder — can prove the finite Commutation Lemma, because satisfiable single-defect witnesses survive each one. The frontier is exactly the **corridor value system** `φ = ψ` on M: minimally unsatisfiable in the observed range — at least one defect always (conjecturally), exactly one sometimes.

Suggested proof engine: the universal L-straddle (φ pulls down, ψ pushes up) combined with the Diagonal Lemma's sign-coupling — together they mean a defect-free corridor would need an orbit alternately descending under φ and ascending under ψ inside the open interval (β, τ), with nowhere to close.
