## 20. Twelfth Exploration Round (v2.3): The Boundary Cell — the Finite Commutation Lemma Proved

The corridor value system of §19.6 is unsatisfiable. This round first maps the corridor's defect geometry (Composite Fixed Point Lemma, zone determinism, the O-class census, the near-miss orbit census), then closes the program's founding question: **on a finite chain, no band-satisfying polar configuration commutes.** The proof is elementary — two pigeonhole staircases meet at one boundary cell.

### 20.1 The Composite Fixed Point Lemma

**Lemma.** For any index i: `φ(i) = i ⟺ ψ(i) = i ⟺ a(i) = b(i) = i`.

*Proof.* (⟸) If a(i) = b(i) = i, then φ(i) = a(b(i)) = i and ψ(i) = b(a(i)) = i. (⟹) Suppose a(b(i)) = i; put z = b(i). a(z) = i means i is a fixed point of row z, i.e. `F(z, i) = i`. b(i) = z means z is a fixed point of column i, i.e. `F(z, i) = z`. The same cell gives i = z, hence b(i) = i and a(i) = a(z) = i. ∎

**Validation.** Exact on all 90,720 chain-5 configurations and ~95,000 direct samples on chains 6–12: zero mismatches among the three predicates. Corollary: at a defect (`φ(i) ≠ ψ(i)`) neither composite equals i — the 'O' signature is impossible at defects; confirmed at scale (zero O signatures among ~26,000 defect observations, this round and §19.5).

### 20.2 Zone determinism (the Corridor Split)

**Theorem.** In a band-satisfying polar config: `I = {i ∈ M : b(i) ∈ B}` is a down-set containing β+1; `J = {i ∈ M : a(i) ∈ T}` is an up-set containing τ−1; V2 ⟺ I ∩ J = ∅. Every corridor point's profile determines its zone: `b(i) ∈ B ⟹ i ∈ I` (profiles MB, BB); `a(i) ∈ T ⟹ i ∈ J` (profiles TM, TT); the free zone `FZ = M \ (I ∪ J)` carries profiles in {BM, MM, MT} only (V1 kills BT).

*Proof.* b is monotone (F monotone in the first argument), so the preimage of the down-set B is a down-set; β+1 ∈ I is the §17 pinning. Dual for J and a⁻¹(T). V2 is exactly the exclusion of (T,B) profiles, i.e. I ∩ J = ∅. On FZ, V1 excludes a(i) ∈ B ∧ b(i) ∈ T. ∎

**Validation.** zoneViol = 0 across ~74,000 V2-clean samples (chains 6–12); FZ profile keys exactly {MM, BM, MT}. **But the free zone is usually empty:** among V2-clean configs, FZ = ∅ in 100% of chain-6 samples (1,849/1,849) and 65–81% on chains 7–12 (e.g. chain 7: 2,767 of 3,398). The §19.6 proof engine via free-zone existence is thereby refuted: a proof of unsatisfiability must handle the I/J-adjacent case — exactly where §20.5 strikes.

### 20.3 The O-class: corridor self-fixed points exist

The census found band-satisfying configs with `i ∈ M`, `a(i) = b(i) = i` — both derived maps fix a corridor point, and φ(i) = ψ(i) = i there, a genuinely commuting corridor index: 68 per 20k samples (chain 7), 98/15k (chain 8), 85/12k (chain 9), 59/10k (chain 10), 32/8k (chain 12). So no per-index pattern is contradictory: **commutation's impossibility is strictly a global phenomenon.** (These O-points stall composite orbits, so any orbit argument must account for them — §20.5 does, via the Composite Fixed Point Lemma.)

Also re-confirmed at scale: no 'H' signature (`ψ(i) < i < φ(i)`) among ~26,000 defects; defect signatures are only L (straddle), BB, AA — the straddle direction is fixed: whenever the μν-composite pulls below, the νμ-composite pushes above.

### 20.4 Near-miss geometry: the defect is the sink/rise seam

Census over **21,674 single-defect configurations** (chains 6, 7, 9, 12; 36,000 fresh direct samples; pinning violations 0; defects outside M: 0):

1. **At the defect: 100% orbit split.** The φ-orbit of the defect index sinks to 0 in every single-defect config (21,674/21,674); the ψ-orbit rises to N in every one.
2. **Below the defect: 100% sinking.** Every corridor point strictly below the defect has a sinking composite orbit (3,527/3,527; zero rises, zero stalls).
3. **Above the defect: 100% rising.** Every corridor point above the defect rises (3,761/3,761; zero sinks, zero stalls).

A near-miss corridor is exactly: **sinking prefix ⊕ defect (φ pulls it below, ψ pushes it above — the L-straddle of §19.5) ⊕ rising suffix.** Non-commutation's unique escape hatch is a single index where φ and ψ disagree — the seam. Commutation closes that hatch, and the corridor must then split between two adjacent indices. This is precisely where the proof of §20.5 lands.

### 20.5 The Finite Commutation Lemma

**Theorem (FCL).** Let F be monotone in both arguments on the finite chain 0 ⊑ … ⊑ N, with derived `a(x) = νY.F(x,Y)`, `b(y) = μX.F(X,y)`. If the configuration is polar (`a(0)=b(0)=0`, `a(N)=b(N)=N`) and band-satisfying (`a(B) ⊆ B`, `b(T) ⊆ T`), then **a∘b ≠ b∘a**.

By the §15 polar reduction and the §16.2 fact that commutation implies the bands, this proves the Finite Commutation Lemma in its original form: *on a finite chain, commutation of a and b forces m = μ(a) = ν(b) = n.* Together with the §16.4 counterexample on [0,1], the Commutation Lemma is now **exactly characterized: true on every finite chain, false on [0,1]** — finiteness is not merely sufficient but the whole story's dividing line.

*Proof.* Suppose `h := a∘b = b∘a`.

1. **Band automaticity (§19.1):** b ≡ 0 on B and a ≡ N on T, with b(T) ⊆ T, a(B) ⊆ B, give `h ≡ 0 on B` and `h ≡ N on T`. Also `b(z) = 0 ⟺ z ∈ B`: b(z) = 0 means column z has a fixed point at 0, i.e. F(0,z) = 0, i.e. z ⊑ β (β is the greatest zero of row 0; the zero set is a down-set by monotonicity).
2. **Fixed points of h:** 0, N, and — by the Composite Fixed Point Lemma (§20.1) — exactly the indices i ∈ M with a(i) = b(i) = i.
3. **Orbits:** h is monotone (composition of monotone maps), so every orbit is monotone and, the chain being finite, stabilizes at a fixed point of h.
4. **Pinning (§17):** `b(β+1) ∈ B` and `a(τ−1) ∈ T`.
5. **The sink set `K := {i ∈ M : the h-orbit of i reaches 0}`.** β+1 ∈ K: h(β+1) = a(b(β+1)) ∈ B, and h ≡ 0 on B. K is a down-set: i ⊑ j ∈ K ⟹ hᵏ(i) ⊑ hᵏ(j) → 0. Hence K = [β+1, s] with s = max K. K is closed under a: h∘a = a∘h holds by pure associativity (h∘a = a∘b∘a = a∘h), so hᵏ(a(s)) = a(hᵏ(s)) → a(0) = 0.
6. **The rise set `L := {i ∈ M : the h-orbit of i reaches N}` contains τ−1** (a(τ−1) ∈ T ⟹ h(τ−1) = b(a(τ−1)) ∈ T ⟹ next step N). K ∩ L = ∅ (one orbit, one fixed point). So s < τ−1 and **s+1 ∈ M**. (On chain 5 the proof ends a step earlier: β+1 = τ−1 = 2, so pinning alone puts 2 ∈ K ∩ L.)
7. **a(s) ⊑ s.** a(s) ∈ T would give h(s) = b(a(s)) ∈ T, putting s ∈ L ∩ K = ∅. Otherwise a(s) ∈ B (⊑ β < s) or a(s) ∈ M ∩ K = [β+1, s] (step 5); either way ⊑ s.
8. **Row-s staircase.** All fixed points of row s lie ⊑ a(s) ⊑ s, so F(s, y) ≠ y for every y ∈ (s, N]. The descent pigeonhole (f = F(s, ·) nondecreasing, f(N) ⊑ N−1, then f(y−1) ⊑ f(y) ⊑ y with f(y−1) ≠ y−1 inductively) yields **F(s, y) ⊑ y−1 for all y ∈ (s, N]**; in particular `F(s, s+1) ⊑ s`.
9. **b(s+1) ⊒ s+1.** If b(s+1) ∈ B, then h(s+1) = a(b(s+1)) ∈ B, so s+1 ∈ K — impossible (s+1 > s). If b(s+1) ∈ M with b(s+1) ⊑ s, then b(s+1) ∈ K, so its orbit reaches 0; using h∘b = b∘h (this is where commutation is used): hᵏ(b(s+1)) = b(hᵏ(s+1)) → 0, so b(hᵏ(s+1)) = 0 for some k, whence hᵏ(s+1) ∈ B by step 1 — putting s+1 ∈ K, impossible. So b(s+1) ∈ T (⊒ τ ⊒ s+1) or b(s+1) ∈ M with b(s+1) ⊒ s+1.
10. **Column-(s+1) staircase.** The least fixed point of column s+1 is b(s+1) ⊒ s+1, so column s+1 has no fixed point ⊑ s. The ascent pigeonhole (g = F(·, s+1) nondecreasing, g(0) ⊒ 1, then g(x) ⊒ g(x−1) ⊒ x with g(x) ≠ x inductively) yields **F(x, s+1) ⊒ x+1 for all x ⊑ s**; in particular `F(s, s+1) ⊒ s+1`.
11. **Contradiction at the boundary cell.** Step 8 gives F(s, s+1) ⊑ s; step 10 gives F(s, s+1) ⊒ s+1. ∎

The near-miss census of §20.4 is the empirical shadow of this proof: a single defect localizes the sink/rise seam *at one index*, where φ ≠ ψ is permitted; commutation forces the seam *between* adjacent indices s, s+1 — and the cell F(s, s+1) is then squeezed from both sides. The proof uses only bands, polar, pinning, the Composite Fixed Point Lemma, and commutation: none of the ladder rungs V2/P/E of §19.3 (each is commutation-implied anyway). Consistency: zero commuting configurations in the exhaustive 90,720-config chain-5 census and ~570,000 direct samples on chains 5–12 across §18–§20.
