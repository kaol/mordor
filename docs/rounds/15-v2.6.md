## 23. Fifteenth Exploration Round (v2.6): The Lattice Frontier — Retracts, the Infinite-Chain Criterion, and a Two-Dimensional Failure

Round fourteen closed the chain problem: a complete chain satisfies CL iff finite. This round takes up §22.6(1), the lattice frontier, and settles its central question negatively. Three results: a **Retract Theorem** transporting configurations from subchains into the ambient lattice; **Theorem A** (Infinite-Chain Criterion): every complete lattice containing an infinite chain fails CL — the infinite world is closed in one stroke; and an explicit **two-dimensional failure on 2^ω** refuting the interval criterion. The surviving frontier is the chain-finite complete lattices, where the recalibrated census finds zero violators through height 4.

### 23.1 The Retract Theorem

**Theorem (Retract).** Let L be a complete lattice and C ⊆ L a complete subchain containing ⊥ and ⊤. Define the *least-above retraction* σ: L → C by σ(x) := min{c ∈ C : x ≤ c}. (The minimum exists: the set is nonempty since ⊤ ∈ C, and its infimum i satisfies x ≤ i — x is a lower bound — so i lies in the set and is least.) Then:

1. σ is a monotone retraction: σ|_C = id, σ(⊥) = ⊥, σ(⊤) = ⊤.
2. For every monotone F₀ : C×C → C, the transport F := F₀ ∘ (σ,σ) is monotone on L×L, and its derived maps are a_F = a₀ ∘ σ and b_F = b₀ ∘ σ.
3. Consequently φ_F = φ₀ ∘ σ and ψ_F = ψ₀ ∘ σ: commutation, polarity, and the fixed points transfer — F commutes iff F₀ does, F is polar iff F₀ is, and m_F = m₀, n_F = n₀.
4. In particular, if C carries a commuting polar configuration (m₀ ≠ n₀), then so does L: the Commutation Lemma fails on L.

*Proof.* Monotonicity: x ≤ x′ implies {c ∈ C : x′ ≤ c} ⊆ {c ∈ C : x ≤ c}, and the minimum of a subset is at least the minimum of a superset; σ|_C = id is immediate in a chain. (2): F(x,y) = F₀(σx, σy) ∈ C for all x, y, so every fixed point of the row map y ↦ F(x,y) lies in C; on C, σ = id, so the row-x fixed points are exactly the fixed points of the row-σ(x) map of F₀, and the greatest of these is a₀(σ(x)); columns dually. (3): b₀(σx) ∈ C and σ|_C = id give σ ∘ b₀ ∘ σ = b₀ ∘ σ, hence φ_F = a₀∘b₀∘σ = φ₀∘σ and ψ_F = ψ₀∘σ. The fixed points of φ_F are C-valued and coincide on C with those of φ₀, so m_F = m₀; dually n_F = n₀. Polarity: a_F(⊥) = a₀(σ(⊥)) = a₀(⊥), and similarly at ⊤. ∎

**Machine validation.** (i) On 2⁴ with the maximal chain C = ∅ ⊂ {0} ⊂ {0,1} ⊂ {0,1,2} ⊂ X: F₀ = min gives a_F = σ, b_F ≡ ∅, commutation exact, m = n = ∅; F₀ = max gives a_F ≡ X, b_F = σ, m = n = X — all identities exact. (ii) **Random transport battery:** 200 trials, each with a uniformly random maximal chain of 2⁴ and F₀ drawn *exactly uniformly* from all monotone bifunctions of the 5-chain (plane-partition DP; the DP totals reproduce the MacMahon census sizes of §16.4 — 16,818,516 on chain-5): monotonicity of the transport, a_F = a₀∘σ, b_F = b₀∘σ, commutation-transfer, polarity-transfer, m_F = m₀, n_F = n₀ held in **200/200 trials, zero failures**. (iii) **Collision rung:** the interior-λ Ladder on the *finite* 5-chain fails to commute at exactly the rung λ−1, whose escape s(λ−1) = λ collides with the limit — the strictness x < s(x) < λ of §22.3 is precisely what infinitude supplies; transported to 2⁴, the mismatch sits exactly on the preimage σ⁻¹(collision rung).

Two remarks. The interval-transport of §22.5.4 is the special case where the failing chain sits inside one interval; the Retract Theorem is strictly more general — C need only be a bounded complete subchain, not an interval. And least-above retractions are standard objects in the theory of retracts of ordered sets (monotone retractions onto chains and posets; order varieties closed under retracts).

### 23.2 Theorem A: the Infinite-Chain Criterion

**Lemma (ω/ω*, DC).** Every infinite chain contains a strictly ascending sequence c₀ < c₁ < ⋯ or a strictly descending sequence c₀ > c₁ > ⋯. *Proof.* If iterated "pick a larger element" never stops, DC delivers an ascending ω-sequence. Otherwise every nonempty subset has a maximum (a subset without one feeds that process), and iterated max-removal builds a strictly descending ω*-sequence. ∎ (The classical monotone-subsequence theorem for linear orders in its general form; it uses dependent choice — without some choice an infinite linear order can be Dedekind-finite.)

**Theorem A (Infinite-Chain Criterion).** *A complete lattice that contains an infinite chain fails the Commutation Lemma.*

*Proof.* Let K ⊆ L be an infinite chain; fix an ascending or descending sequence (cₙ) in K by the Lemma.

*Ascending case.* Let z′ := ⋁ₙ cₙ (completeness of L). Then z′ > cₙ for all n (if z′ = cₙ then cₙ₊₁ ≤ z′ = cₙ < cₙ₊₁). Set C := {⊥} ∪ {cₙ} ∪ {z′, ⊤}: a chain ⊥ ≤ c₀ ≤ c₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ z′ ≤ ⊤. C is a complete chain in its own right: a subset of {cₙ} either has a greatest element (index set with maximum) or is cofinal in {cₙ}, with supremum z′ ∈ C; infima are minima, c₀, or ⊥; supersets containing z′ or ⊤ are handled at the top. And C is infinite.

*Descending case.* Let z := ⋀ₙ cₙ. Then z < cₙ for all n (if z = cₙ then z ≤ cₙ₊₁ < cₙ = z). Set C := {⊥, z} ∪ {cₙ} ∪ {⊤}: a chain ⊥ ≤ z ≤ ⋯ ≤ c₁ ≤ c₀ ≤ ⊤, complete (any subset of the cₙ's has a greatest element — the indices descend; suprema are such maxima, z, or ⊤; infima are z, minima, or ⊥), and infinite.

In both cases C is an **infinite complete subchain of L containing ⊥_L and ⊤_L**. By the Sharpness Theorem (§22.5), C carries a commuting polar configuration F₀ with m₀ = ⊥ ≠ ⊤ = n₀ — and the bounds of C are those of L. By the Retract Theorem (§23.1), F := F₀∘(σ,σ) is a commuting polar configuration on L with m = m₀ ≠ n₀ = n. ∎

**Corollary (the infinite world is closed).** A complete lattice satisfies the Commutation Lemma only if it is chain-finite. Theorem A subsumes the interval-negative class of §22.5.4 (an infinite chain interval is an infinite chain) and completes the classification of the infinite side: every CL-positive complete lattice is chain-finite, and among chains exactly the finite ones qualify. The M_κ diamonds (§16.2) remain the standing infinite positive examples; any other must be chain-finite.

**Remark (the retract route is closed on chain-finite lattices).** If L is chain-finite, every subchain is finite, so by the Finite Commutation Lemma (§20.5) no subchain carries a commuting polar configuration, and no retract-transport from a subchain can fail. The *only known failure mechanism* — retraction from an infinite chain — is unavailable on chain-finite lattices. This is structural evidence for, not a proof of, the finite Commutation Lemma: a failure on a chain-finite lattice would have to be essentially two-dimensional (§23.3 shows two-dimensional failures exist among general complete lattices; whether any can be chain-finite is the open question).

### 23.3 The two-dimensional failure on 2^ω: the interval criterion refuted

§22.6(1) asked whether every failure is witnessed by a failing chain *interval*. The answer is no: the witness is a chain *retract*.

**The configuration.** Let X = {0,1,2,…}, L = 2^X, Sₙ = {0,…,n−1} (S₀ = ∅), and C = {Sₙ : n < ω} ∪ {X} — the chain of initial segments plus the top, ≅ ω+1, a complete subchain of L containing ∅ and X. The least-above retraction is σ(U) = min{c ∈ C : U ⊆ c}: σ(U) = S_{max(U)+1} for nonempty finite U, σ(∅) = ∅, and σ(U) = X iff U is infinite. Let F₀ be the §21.3 Example (i) configuration on C (the Ladder at λ = X with rungs Sₙ ↦ Sₙ₊₁), and F := F₀∘(σ,σ). By the Retract Theorem, with a₀ = id:

- a(U) = σ(U) — the least initial segment covering U;
- b(V) = ∅ if V is finite, X if V is infinite — the **infinite detector**;
- φ = ψ exactly, both the infinite detector itself (each composite collapses to "∅ on finite input, X on infinite input") — commutation is exact, not asymptotic;
- polar (a, b fix ∅ and X), and **m = ∅ ≠ X = n**.

So **2^ω fails CL**. The failure is genuinely two-dimensional: the fibers of φ = ψ are the finite/infinite split of 2^X — a proper class-2 map on a width-continuum lattice, not the step of any chain interval.

**No failing chain interval exists.** Every interval of 2^ω has the form [A,B] ≅ 2^(B∖A); this is a chain only when |B∖A| ≤ 1, so every chain interval has at most two elements and satisfies CL trivially. (Machine check on 2⁴: all 81 intervals are ≅ 2^d by rank difference d; the 48 chain intervals have ≤ 2 elements — the same structure as 2^ω.) The failure is witnessed by the subchain C ≅ ω+1 — a retract, not an interval. **The interval criterion of §22.6(1) is refuted; the corrected criterion is Theorem A: the witness is an infinite chain, realized as a retract.**

**Atom/coatom obstructions (why the retraction route is essential).** The [0,1] example (§16.1) took a = b = id. On 2^X neither is possible in a polar configuration: a = id forces F(U,X) ∈ (U,X) for every U < X — boundary repulsion gives F(U,X) ≻ U, while F(U,X) = X would make X a row fixed point above U — but U = X∖{i} is a coatom and (U,X) = ∅. Dually, b = id needs (⊥,V) ≠ ∅ for all V ≻ ⊥, which the atoms {i} forbid. (A dense chain like [0,1] has neither atoms nor coatoms, which is why the identity pair was available there.) The transport a = σ, b = infinite detector is exactly the workaround: composing with σ bypasses both obstructions.

**The 2^k seam test (machine-verified).** Truncate to 2^k: segments S₀,…,S_{k−1}, X = {0,…,k−1}; σ(U) = S_{max(U)+1} for max(U) ≤ k−2, and σ(U) = X iff k−1 ∈ U — the truncation of "infinite" is "contains the last element". F₀ is Example (i) clamped, the escape of the last rung forced up to X (the clamp rung). For k = 2, 3, 4, 5 the machine confirms: F is monotone; b is the **(k−1)-detector** (∅ if k−1 ∉ V, X if k−1 ∈ V) — the exact truncation-mirror of the infinite detector; a(U) = σ(U) below the clamp and X from max(U) = k−2 on; and commutation fails **exactly** on the rows with max(U) = k−2 — the preimage σ⁻¹(S_{k−1}) of the clamp rung — in counts 1, 2, 4, 8. On 2² the mismatch is exactly A = {0}, as computed by hand. Each 2^k has m = ∅ ≠ X = n but φ ≠ ψ, so there is no CL violation (CL constrains only commuting pairs): the Finite Commutation Lemma bites precisely at the artificial seam, and the infinite 2^ω — which has no clamp rung — commutes. This is the two-dimensional counterpart of the ω+1 surrogate phenomenon of §21.3. Corollary: on 2^4, every retraction-transported configuration either commutes with m = n (FCL on the finite retract chain) or fails to commute at a clamp seam — the transport family *provably* contains no B4 violator; a B4 violator, if one exists, must be invisible to retraction from subchains.

### 23.4 The chain-finite frontier and the recalibrated census

The frontier: **which chain-finite complete lattices satisfy CL?** Two parts. (a) The finite Commutation Lemma at height ≥ 4 (proven: chains, §20.5; height ≤ 3, §16.2). (b) Infinite chain-finite lattices: M_κ (positive, No-Room) and the new **E_κ column lattices** — ⊥, ⊤, and κ independent columns aᵢ < bᵢ < cᵢ with cross-column joins ⊤ and meets ⊥. E_κ is complete, chain-finite (chains ≤ 5 elements), of **height 4** — the first height beyond No-Room — and infinite when κ is. E₁ is the 5-chain (CL ✓); E₂, E₃ are finite and open; E_ω is an infinite chain-finite test case.

**Sampler recalibration.** The v2.5 session's greedy samplers reported 100% commuting on chain-4 against a 50.4% exhaustive truth — an artifact of that implementation. This round rebuilds and calibrates the samplers on chain-4, where the exhaustive ground truth is available (24,696 bifunctions, 12,450 commuting, 7,280 with m ≠ n, 0 polar violators — matching §15.1/§22.1):

| Sampler (20,000 draws each) | commuting | m ≠ n | mean cell value |
|---|---|---|---|
| greedy-up / greedy-down | 93.0% / 93.2% | 1.9% / 1.8% | 2.72 / 0.28 |
| envelope-up / envelope-down | 87.6% / 87.5% | 2.0% / 2.1% | 2.61 / 0.39 |
| exact uniform (plane-partition DP) | 50.8% | 29.1% | 1.50 |
| exhaustive truth | 50.4% | 29.5% | — |

The heuristics under-represent m≠n-structure by ≈15× but are no longer degenerate; exact uniform sampling exists for chains only (the row-state DP does not factor on non-chain lattices, where rows are monotone self-maps coupled through pointwise domination).

**Census (machine, this round).** Four monotone-by-construction samplers (greedy-up/down, envelope-up/down; construction-verified monotone), derived maps by Tarski iteration; heights follow the §16.2 convention:

Zero commuting violators everywhere, and the controls validate the machinery: on M3 (proven CL by No-Room) the samplers *do* surface its 239 non-commuting polar m≠n-configurations while reporting zero commuting violators — the census is not blind to the phenomenon class. Two honest caveats. First, the B4 row is the weakest: all four samplers produced 100% commuting configurations there (the join-rich Boolean structure makes heuristic values inflate toward near-constant maps). Second, heuristic coverage is uneven (the ≈15× calibration factor), so the zeros are confirmatory, not probative. The structural support is §23.2's remark — the known failure mechanism (retraction from an infinite chain) is closed on chain-finite lattices — plus §23.3's corollary that the transport family provably contains no B4 violator.

### 23.5 Open problems (after round fifteen)

1. **The chain-finite question.** Does every chain-finite complete lattice satisfy the Commutation Lemma? Split: (a) finite-CL at height ≥ 4 — B4 and E₂, E₃ censused clean this round under biased samplers, proven only for chains and height ≤ 3; (b) infinite chain-finite lattices — the E_κ column lattices (height 4) are the minimal open class, and by Theorem A every infinite CL-positive lattice must live here. **Resolution (v2.7, §24): part (a) advances for E₂** — the pair-level method gives a complete machine proof that **CL holds on E₂** (§24.5, Theorem B): all 4,356² polar pairs scanned, every commuting pair refuted or unrealizable, zero witnesses. This is the first CL-positive result at height 4 beyond chains. E₃, B4, and part (b) remain open.
2. **Census continuation.** Extend exact or near-uniform sampling beyond chains (uniform monotone bifunctions on a non-chain lattice have no known factorization); push the B4/E_κ census with structured samplers targeting near-polar shapes (e.g., prescribed a, b with realizability search).
3. **Failure zone** (unchanged, §15.3–15.4): structural characterization of non-iso δ₀ beyond the two degenerating positive classes.

Literature notes (plain references): monotone retractions and retracts of ordered sets are classical in the theory of order retracts (retracts of chains; order varieties closed under retracts) — the least-above retraction of §23.1 is standard there; the general monotone-subsequence theorem for linear orders (every infinite linearly ordered set contains a subset of order type ω or ω*, under dependent choice) underlies the Lemma of §23.2; MacMahon's boxed plane partitions give the census sizes (§16.4); the fixed-point backdrop remains Knaster–Tarski and Cousot–Cousot as in §§20–21.
