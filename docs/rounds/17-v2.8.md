## 25. Seventeenth Exploration Round (v2.8): The One-Way Road — The E₂ Audit, the Pentagon Erratum, the Clean Block-Diagonal Family on E₃, and the Pullback Gap

Round sixteen proved Theorem B (CL holds on E₂) by a complete pair scan. This round does three things: it **audits** that theorem end-to-end with an independently rebuilt solver (§25.1), corrects an error in the round-16 validation table discovered by the audit's cross-checks (§25.2), and attacks the top open problem — the lifting question "does E₂-CL imply E_κ-CL?" — with a complete scan of the block-diagonal family on E₃ (§25.4) and a transport analysis (§25.5) that closes the naive route in both directions: the answer is a **one-way road**. Failure propagates upward along the merge retractions (E_j fails ⟹ E_κ fails, j ≤ κ); positivity does not propagate at all — the pullback of fixed points and of commutation along a retraction fails in general, by explicit counterexamples and exhaustive rates.

### 25.4 The block-diagonal family on E₃ is clean

Call a pair (a, b) on E_κ *block-diagonal* if each column's three elements are mapped within the same column (a column-wise 5-chain action per column). Three facts make the family completely scannable.

**Decomposition Lemma.** For block-diagonal pairs, commutation and all five filters decompose per column: commutation a∘b = b∘a holds on E_κ iff it holds within each column; each cover of E_κ involves a single column plus the bounds (a(⊥) = ⊥ and b(⊤) = ⊤ always), so F3 is a per-column condition; F4′/F5 are per-element; and F1/F2 (identity pairs) die per column through F3 at the (coatom, ⊤) and (⊥, atom) covers.

*Proof sketch.* For commutation, both a(b(x)) and b(a(x)) stay in x's column, and ⊥, ⊤ are fixed; the covers of E_κ are exactly (⊥, aᵢ), (aᵢ, bᵢ), (bᵢ, cᵢ), (cᵢ, ⊤) per column. The filter conditions are instances of the global lemmas whose hypotheses reference single elements or single covers. ∎

**Per-column census** (on the 5-chain, §25.3): 35 maps, 351 commuting pairs, of which **84 survive** the per-column F3/F4′/F5 conditions. The block-diagonal family on E₃ therefore has 84³ = **592,704 commuting pairs**, all surviving the filters.

**Symmetry.** The group S₃ (column permutations — automorphisms of E₃) × C₂ (the within-column mirror anti-automorphism δ, acting on pairs as (a, b) ↦ (δbδ, δaδ), a per-column involution on the 84 survivors, verified as such) acts on the family; realizability, commutation, and polarity are invariant, so only canonical representatives need solving: the lex-min of (sorted triple, sorted mirror) — 51,213 canonical triples.

**Result.** All 51,213 canonical triples solved: **every one refuted in pure propagation** (zero search nodes, no caps hit; the average refutation takes ≈122 edge revisions), zero witnesses. Sampled global pairs verified commuting and polar (20/20). Control: the same family on E₂ (84² = 7,056 pairs, all commuting, all refuted, zero nodes) is a subset of the audited Theorem B scan — the E₃ solver's 'no' answers cross-validate against the audited E₂ results.

**Corollary (structural restriction).** Any CL violator on E₃ must be *cross-column*: its derived maps must move points between columns. This is the first structural restriction on E₃ violators, and it sharpens the comparability gap (§24.6): the obstruction to proving E_κ-CL lives, concretely, in the cross-column cells of the grid — exactly where the chain theory has no grip.

### 25.5 The One-Way Road: transport along retractions

**Upward (pushforward) — always available.** The §23.1 Retract Theorem argument uses only that m: L → C is a monotone retraction (m∘s = id, m|=C = id): for F₀ on C, the transport F₀∘(m,m) on L has derived maps (a₀∘m, b₀∘m), so every configuration — commuting, polar, m ≠ n — lifts from C to L. Machine check: 300/300 random transports (150 E₂←5-chain, 150 E₃←E₂-merge) transfer derived maps, commutation, polarity, and the fixed points exactly. The *merge retractions* E_κ → E_j (merge κ−j columns into one) exist for all 2 ≤ j ≤ κ and for E_ω. Consequence — the **ordering of the frontier**:

> E_j carries a violator ⟹ E_κ carries a violator, for all 2 ≤ j ≤ κ (and E_ω). Contrapositive: E_κ-CL ⟹ E_j-CL.

E₂ is thus the base of the implication chain — Theorem B was the forced first target — and E₃ is now the genuine frontier: E₃-CL would re-confirm E₂-CL but imply nothing about E₄.

**No classical downward route.** Among *all* 287,496 monotone bound-preserving maps E₃ → E₂ (a complete census: 66 per-column weak 3-chains in E₂, three independent columns), exactly **zero are lattice homomorphisms** (join- and meet-preserving). There is no homomorphic retraction E₃ → E₂, so no classical fixed-point-transport route downward exists.

**The Pullback Gap — downward is blocked twice over.** The naive lifting route asks: given F on E₃ with derived pair (a, b), is the derived pair of F′ = m∘F∘(s,s) on E₂ equal to (m∘a∘s, m∘b∘s)? Two necessary steps, both false in general.

*(i) Fixed-point transport fails.* Even the single-map identity ν(m∘f∘s) = m(νf) is false. Hand counterexamples, machine-verified. On E₂ with the least-above retraction onto the 5-chain: f = [⊥, a₀, b₀, c₀, a₀, a₀, a₀, a₁] is monotone, νf = c₀, m(νf) = c₀ — but the pulled map m∘f∘s is the *identity* on the chain, so ν(m∘f∘s) = ⊤. On the merge E₃ → E₂: f = (·)∧c₂ is monotone, νf = c₂, m(νf) = c₁ — but ν(m∘f∘s) = ⊥. Exhaustive rates (complete enumeration of all monotone self-maps; the pulled side computed exactly):

| L, retraction | maps | ν-transport violated | μ-transport violated |
|---|---|---|---|
| E₂, least-above onto 5-chain | 45,540 | 9,856 (21.6%) | 2,004 (4.4%) |
| M3, least-above onto 3-chain | 371 | 54 (14.6%) | 20 (5.4%) |
| pentagon N5, least-above onto 4-chain | *withdrawn, §26.2* | — | — |
| E₃, merge onto E₂ | 3,000,000 (DFS-prefix sample) | 675,270 (22.5%) | 0 in-prefix |

*(ii) Commutation transport fails.* Even where derived maps transported, the pulled pair need not commute. Exhaustive: on M3, 426 of 7,597 commuting pairs (140 of the 1,643 polar ones) lose commutation under pullback; on the pentagon, 292 of 4,616 (66 of 851 polar — *pentagon figures withdrawn, §26.2: computed from the flawed v2.8 enumeration*). So the lifting route fails at both steps, for chain retractions and for the merge alike.

**Methodological note (the sampling mirage).** Greedy random batteries return *zero* violations in 6,000 trials on the merge and 300 on the chain retraction — the failure patterns require high images under low fixed points (e.g., f(⊤) landing mid-column while all fixed points sit low), precisely what greedy-up construction suppresses. The §23.4 lesson recurs at transport level: biased construction can mask structure by orders of magnitude; exhaustive enumeration and exact CSP are the trustworthy instruments.

**Consequence.** The lifting theorem "E₂-CL ⟹ E₃-CL" is unavailable by the natural route. E₃ stands on its own evidence: the block-diagonal family is clean (§25.4), the only known failure mechanism is closed on chain-finite lattices (§23.2), and the comparability gap (§24.6) explains why. A proof of E_κ-CL, if one exists, must be genuinely two-dimensional.

### 25.6 Open problems (after round seventeen)

1. **E₃, cross-column pairs.** The block-diagonal family is clean; the violator, if any, lives in the cross-column part of the 912,673² pair space. Next: structured cross-column families (e.g., pairs moving only column 2 into columns 0–1), or a theory of cross-column obstruction to sharpen the comparability gap into a filter.
2. **The transport question (new).** Characterize exactly which retractions admit pullback transport of ν, μ, and commutation. The exhaustive rates (21.6%/4.4% ν/μ on E₂-chain) suggest a graded, structural obstruction; a lattice-theoretic characterization would either yield a corrected lifting theorem or a proof that the one-way road is intrinsic.
3. **E_ω and the infinite chain-finite class.** By the pushforward ordering, E_ω fails whenever E₂ fails — the infinite test case is reduced, in the failure direction, to the same frontier (and Theorem B already covers its base).
4. **B4 = 2⁴** (unchanged): infeasible by pair scan; transport-family-free by §23.3; no lifting from E₂ available by §25.5.
5. **The chain-finite conjecture** (standing, strengthened): every chain-finite complete lattice satisfies CL. Proven classes: finite chains (FCL), height ≤ 3 (No-Room), E₂ (Theorem B). Clean families: E₃ block-diagonal (this round). The frontier order of §25.5 says: prove E₃ next, or find the cross-column violator.
6. **Failure zone** (unchanged, §15.3–15.4).
