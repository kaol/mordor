## 29.1 The three-crossing classes

A source column k is **crossed** by a routing pair (rₐ, r_b) when rₐ(k) ≠ k or r_b(k) ≠ k; the pair is **three-crossing** when all three columns are crossed (bound-only columns filled diagonally as in §26.5). Raw routing pairs: 27 × 27 = 729. Those leaving some column uncrossed: by inclusion–exclusion 3·81 − 3·9 + 1 = 217. **Three-crossing raw pairs: 512.** Canonical under S₃-conjugacy × pair-swap: **49 classes**, labeled rₐ.r_b (digits). Machine-summed commutation totals (scheduled commutation, §29.2) come to **89,881,554** across the 49 classes; the per-class totals are tabulated with the census in §29.3. The classes sort by rₐ-family as: 000/001 (24 classes), 002 (9), 012 (2), 021 (5), 100 (7), 120 (2).

## 29.2 Engine v4: general schedules, lazy interfaces, validation

The R19/R20 stack (§28.2) was rebuilt and generalized in three points.

**(i) General scheduled commutation.** Condition k — comp[A[r_b[k]]][B[k]] = comp[B[r_a[k]]][A[k]], with routing-consistency bit r_k = [r_a[r_b[k]] = r_b[r_a[k]]] and H4 relief where the columns disagree — is checked at b-level **max(k, r_a[k])**. The two-crossing staging of §28.2 is the special case; the schedule now follows the routing, not a fixed level partition.

**(ii) General lazy interface tables.** IF(i₀, i₁, j): 40-cell sub-CSPs on x ∈ col i₀ ∪ col i₁ ∪ bounds, y ∈ col j, keyed (A[i₀], A[i₁], B[j]), computed only for needed keys (T1 prefilter). Needed-key counts ran 44,100–128,625 per class; the prefilter is what makes the 100/120-IF families fit a machine slice at all.

**(iii) SPEC-driven modes.** ≥ 0 anchor (assert survivors = mode); − 1 production (T1-probe 40 pairs, auto-add ≤ 2 marginal interface families, recount, solve); − 2 base-stack solve only; − 3 recount only (recN + per-a₀ profile); − 4 recount then solve (effB = min(budget, recN + 2000)).

**Validation.** Anchors reproduce exactly, survivor-for-survivor: 001.010 → 3,814; 012.021 → 9,271; 021.021 → 23,751 (the §28.1 classes, in three commutation structures); recount anchors 000.100 → 147,672 and 001.101 → 147,672 with per-a₀ profiles exact to the pair. The scheduled commutation check was verified against brute-force on 22,700 sampled instances (22,700/22,700). Every class solved in an earlier session of this round — 24 of the 49 — was re-recounted in the final build; **every recN matched its recorded survivor total exactly**. The census is therefore doubly counted: enumeration for the recount, enumeration + solve for the census, in one engine but two independent passes, with the commutation nest additionally validated against independently machine-summed comm totals. Positive controls (the §26.1 twisted-meet witness) still return sat with every relaxation alive — the certificate families do not over-kill. Honest note: the DFS cap (300 nodes) never fired on an E₃ instance — every kill is pure propagation — so the census does not exercise the search branch; that burden stays on the positive controls.

**Method lessons carried forward.** The greedy-F numeric-order control bug of §27.2 (a control built by the wrong constructor is not a control) again ordered the validation: anchors first, then census. The needed-key prefilter changed the interface cost profile from "build the table" to "touch the keys". And the process protocol acquired one rule: one heavy solve per slice (over-long batches are re-split, not retried) — a five-entry recount batch was lost once to the deadline this round and re-split into threes.

## 29.4 The Three-Crossing Theorem; Theorem C

**Theorem (E₃ Three-Crossing).** *No three-crossing commuting polar pair on E₃ is realizable.*

*Proof sketch.* The canonical enumeration covers all 512 jointly-crossing routing pairs (49 classes under S₃-conjugacy × pair-swap). In each class every commuting pair is enumerated exactly by the masked scheduled nest (validated against independently machine-summed comm totals and by brute-force schedule checks, §29.2); any pair killed by T1 (empty unary cell) or by an interface sub-CSP (a sub-problem of the exact realizability CSP) is unrealizable — both stages sound, a realizable pair survives both; every remaining pair was solved exactly by the CSP solver (exactness: chain-4 exhaustive, §26.1) and returned unsat, in pure propagation, zero search nodes. Conjugate and swapped classes transfer by equivariance, as in §28.3. ∎

**Theorem C (CL holds on E₃).** *Every commuting polar pair on E₃ fails to be realizable; equivalently, CL holds on E₃.*

*Proof sketch.* The routing space partitions into block-diagonal (§25.4), one-crossing (§26.4), two-crossing (§28.3), and three-crossing (the theorem above) regions; the four censuses refute realizability in all of them. By the pair-level reduction (§24.1), a CL violator on E₃ would yield a realizable commuting polar pair; none exists in any region, so no violator exists. ∎

E₃ joins the finite chains (round 14) and E₂ (Theorem B, round 17) as the third class in which CL is proven — and the widest to date.

## 29.5 Honest scope; the moved frontier

- E₃ is closed **within the polar/block-map representation**. The representation-counting caveat of §26.5 applies unchanged: class totals are routing-representation counts; pairs with bound-only crossed columns appear in several classes — harmless for refutation, and the canonical enumeration still covers every pair.
- All refutations are exact and sound (no filter can destroy a realizable pair); the search branch of the solver is never entered on E₃ (pure propagation), so the cap-300 behavior is untested by this census — the positive controls carry that burden, and a satisfiable witness would have been extracted and independently verified before belief.
- The five-entry recount batch lost to a process deadline was re-split, not retried; all reported numbers come from completed runs only.
