# Twenty-Second Exploration Round (v3.3): The Cut — a Direct SAT Decision, the Semi-Commutation Lemma, and the Cut Lemma Frontier

Rounds 16–21 decided CL on E₂ and E₃ by censusing commuting polar pairs and refuting realizability pair by pair. This round replaces the census by a single exact SAT instance per lattice (§30.1), which independently confirms Theorems B and C and settles E₄, E₅ and B₄; records errata found by the cross-check (§30.2); proves a strictly stronger, much shorter form of the Finite Commutation Lemma on chains — one-sided **semi-commutation** b∘a ⊑ a∘b already suffices (§30.3); reduces the finite lattice problem, and the chain-finite conjecture, to a single order-theoretic statement, the **Cut Lemma** (§30.4); and gives two reductions on the infinite side — the **Escape Lemma** and a **Herbrand reduction** for lattices of bounded height (§30.5). Scripts: `experiments/sat/`.

## 30.1 The direct SAT decision

**Encoding.** The Realizability Criterion of §24.1 (pins, the two strong exclusions, monotonicity along cover arcs) is encoded one-hot as CNF over the cells F(x, y) *and* over the derived maps a, b themselves, together with polarity and a∘b = b∘a (auxiliary one-hot vectors for the two composites). The pair is no longer enumerated: it is a variable of the instance. One instance per interval of L decides CL on L (see §30.2(i) for why intervals). Every satisfying assignment is decoded and re-derived by an independent semantic checker (greatest/least fixed points computed by brute force), which must confirm monotonicity, a = a_F, b = b_F, and m ≠ n.

**Calibration against the log's ground truth** (`crosscheck.py`, part 1): MacMahon counts 175 / 24,696 / 16,818,516 / 55,197,331,332 (n = 3..6, §16.4); chain-3: 27 derived pairs, 137 commuting, 20 with m ≠ n; chain-4: 24,696 bifunctions, **223 derived pairs**, 12,450 commuting, 7,280 with m ≠ n, 6,440 polar, 0 CL violators (§11, §15, §22.1, §24.1, all exact); the SAT-enumerated set of realizable pairs on chain-4 is **set-equal** to the 223 derived pairs; the twisted-meet control (σ, const-0) on E₂ is found by both formulas recorded in the log (σ(x∧σ(y)) of §24.3 and σ(x)∧y of §26.1); the σ-diagonal is refuted.

## 30.2 Errata and corrections

**(i) The Pair-Level Reduction must quantify over intervals.** §24.1 states: CL fails on L iff L carries a realizable commuting polar pair. The (⟹) proof says "a violator's derived pair is commuting and polar" — but a violator is only polar on [m, n]. The correct statement:

> **CL fails on L ⟺ some interval [z, w] (z < w) of L carries a realizable commuting polar pair.**

(⟹) is the polar reduction (§15.1). (⟸): the clamp r(x) = (x ∨ z) ∧ w is a monotone retraction of L onto [z, w], and the Retract argument (§23.1, valid for any monotone retraction, §25.5) transports a polar configuration G on [z, w] to G∘(r, r) on L with derived maps a_G∘r, b_G∘r, commuting, with m = z ≠ w = n. Theorems B and C are unaffected: every proper interval of E_κ is a chain (machine-checked for κ ≤ 4; in general, an interval not containing both ⊥ and ⊤ lies inside one column plus a bound), so the Finite Commutation Lemma covers it.

**(ii) "No-Room: CL on all complete lattices of height ≤ 3" overstates §16.2.** The No-Room argument needs both rooms around c = F(⊥, ⊤) empty; it therefore covers exactly the lattices whose middle elements are all atom-and-coatom (M_κ, edge-height ≤ 2). From §23 on the log measures height in edges (M₃ = 2, B₃ = 3, E₂ = 4) while keeping "≤ 3", and lists B₃ as "proven (No-Room)" — but the No-Room hypothesis holds at **no** element of B₃ (every atom has a nonempty upper room, every coatom a nonempty lower room; machine-checked). Status after this round: edge-height ≤ 2, *including infinite lattices*, is re-proved by the Herbrand route (§30.5); finite lattices of edge-height 3 (B₃, N5, chain-4, D_k for k ≤ 8, …) are decided by §30.1; **infinite lattices of edge-height 3 (D_ω, M_ω × C₂, …) are open** — the infinite frontier sits at height 3, not at E_ω.

**(iii) Arithmetic.** Round 20a's five classes (§28.2 ‡: 34,143 + 15,478 + 3,814 + 9,299 + 9,271) sum to **72,005**, not 71,005; the two-crossing refutation total is therefore **457,850**, not 456,850 (the per-class entries of `data/e3-two-crossing-census.json` already sum to 457,850; its `totals` and `per_round_refutations` fields carried the slip and are corrected in this round). In §29.3, the 120.201 slice is 96,012 + **43,924** = 139,936 (as in `data/e3-three-crossing-census.json`), not + 43,936. No per-class figure is affected.

**(iv) Cross-references.** Round 21 attributes the finite chains to round 14 and Theorem B to round 17; they are round 12 (§20.5) and round 16 (§24.5, audited in round 17). The top-level README (status, section references, the description of E₂ and of the chain boundary) is corrected in this round.

## 30.3 The Semi-Commutation Lemma on finite chains

The near-miss census of §19.5 found defects only with signature φ(i) < i < ψ(i) (φ = a∘b, ψ = b∘a), never the mirror. That asymmetry is a theorem:

**Theorem (Semi-Commutation, finite chains).** Let F be monotone on a finite chain, a(x) = νY.F(x, Y), b(y) = μX.F(X, y). If **b∘a ⊑ a∘b** pointwise, then μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F.

*Proof.* Suppose m < n. Both are common fixed points (L1, L2), the interval [m, n] is F-stable and carries the restricted derived maps (§15.1), and b∘a ⊑ a∘b restricts; so assume the configuration polar on 0 < … < N. Let D = {x : bᵏ(x) = 0 for some k}.

1. D is a down-set (b monotone), 0 ∈ D, and N ∉ D (b(N) = N ≠ 0). If b(z) ∈ D then z ∈ D.
2. a(D) ⊆ D, by induction on the least k with bᵏ(x) = 0: for k = 0, a(0) = 0. For k > 0, b(x) ∈ D at depth k − 1, so a(b(x)) ∈ D; then b(a(x)) ⊑ a(b(x)) puts b(a(x)) in the down-set D, and step 1 gives a(x) ∈ D.
3. Let d = max D < N. Then a(d) ∈ D, so a(d) ≤ d; and d + 1 ∉ D, so b(d + 1) ∉ D, i.e. b(d + 1) ≥ d + 1.
4. The cell F(d, d+1): if F(d, d+1) ≥ d + 1, then d + 1 is a post-fixed point of row d, so a(d) ≥ d + 1 (Tarski: the greatest fixed point is the join of post-fixed points). Otherwise F(d, d+1) ≤ d, so d is a pre-fixed point of column d + 1, and b(d + 1) ≤ d. Both contradict step 3. ∎

Commutation is the case ψ = φ, so this is a proof of the Finite Commutation Lemma (§20.5) — without bands, pinning, the corridor, or the composite orbit h. The sink set is that of **b alone**; commutation (or semi-commutation) is used once, to make that sink set a-closed. On an infinite complete chain step 3 fails exactly when D has no maximum, which is the Ladder mechanism of §22.3 (D = ↓λ ∖ {λ}).

**Evidence beyond chains.** With b∘a ⊑ a∘b in place of commutation the SAT instance is unsat on every lattice of §30.1's first rows and on all 179 nontrivial lattices with ≤ 4 join-irreducibles (`semicomm.py`). The mirror hypothesis a∘b ⊑ b∘a is satisfiable everywhere — e.g. a = [0, 2, 2], b = [0, 0, 2] on the 3-chain — so the direction is essential, and it points the same way as I1.

## 30.4 The Cut Lemma

The chain proof used totality exactly once (step 4: F(d, d+1) ≤ d or ≥ d + 1). Isolating it:

**Cut Lemma (conjecture for finite lattices).** Let L be a complete lattice, F : L×L → L monotone, D a nonempty proper down-set, U = L ∖ D. Then there exist x ∈ D, y ∈ U with **F(x, y) ≤ x or F(x, y) ≥ y**. Equivalently (Tarski): it is impossible that a(D) ⊆ D and b(U) ⊆ U.

**Theorem (Cut ⟹ Semi-CL).** If the Cut Lemma holds on every interval of a complete lattice L, then on L: b∘a ⊑ a∘b ⟹ μX.νY.F = νY.μX.F; in particular CL holds on L.

*Proof.* Reduce to a polar interval as in §30.3 and take D = {x : bᵏ(x) = ⊥ for some k < ω}. Steps 1–2 hold verbatim in any complete lattice, so a(D) ⊆ D and b(U) ⊆ U, contradicting the Cut Lemma on the interval. ∎

**Proposition (principal cuts).** The Cut Lemma holds if U = ↑u and L has no infinite ascending chain, or dually if D = ↓d and L has no infinite descending chain.

*Proof (U = ↑u).* Suppose no witness. τ(x) = F(x, x ∨ u) is monotone. Iterate from ⊥ ∈ D. If the iteration stabilizes inside D at x = τ(x), then F(x, x ∨ u) = x ≤ x with x ∨ u ∈ U, a witness. Otherwise some x ∈ D has w = τ(x) ∈ U with w ≥ x (the iteration ascends); then w ≥ u since U = ↑u, so w ≥ x ∨ u, and (x, x ∨ u) is a witness. ∎

On a finite chain every down-set is principal, which recovers §30.3. On ω+1 the Ladder configuration has U = {ω} principal but violates the ascending-chain hypothesis — the iteration's limit has no last step inside D — so the proposition is sharp in the same place as the Sharpness Theorem.

**Machine evidence** (`cutlemma.py`). No counterexample in **4,685** (lattice, down-set) instances — every down-set of every lattice with ≤ 4 join-irreducibles, and of E₃ — and none in **50,635** further instances (4,611 random 5-generator lattices of 5–14 elements, up to 12 random down-sets each). **The cover-local strengthening is false:** requiring a witness among cover pairs x ⋖ y fails in 4,030 of the 4,685 instances, first on B₂ with D = {⊥} (F(⊥, p) = q, F(⊥, q) = p, F(⊥, ⊤) ∈ {p, q} — contradiction only via F(⊥, ⊤) ≥ p ∨ q, a join). So a proof must be global, using joins of values along U, as the B₂ case does. Partial reductions available for a minimal counterexample: it may be taken polar (interval [μ(a), ν(b)]); if some x ∈ D ∖ {⊥} has x ≤ F(x, x), then [x, ⊤] is F-stable and carries the restricted cut, a smaller counterexample — so x ≰ F(x, x) on D ∖ {⊥}, and dually F(y, y) ≰ y on U ∖ {⊤}; in particular the diagonal x ↦ F(x, x) fixes only ⊥ and ⊤. These interval reductions lower the height, so an argument completing them would also cover infinite lattices of finite height.

**Consequence.** The Cut Lemma for chain-finite complete lattices implies the chain-finite conjecture, in the stronger semi-commutation form.

## 30.5 The infinite side: Escape and Herbrand

**Escape Lemma.** Let F be a violator (commuting or semi-commuting) on a complete lattice, reduced to a polar interval, and let S be the closure of {⊥, ⊤} under F, a, b, ∨, ∧. If S is finite, it is a finite sublattice on which pins and both exclusions still hold (they are universal statements), so by the Realizability Criterion the derived maps of F|S are a|S, b|S, and S carries a violator. Hence, **if CL (or semi-CL) holds on all finite lattices, every violator generates an infinite structure from its bounds.** On chains that structure is the ladder; at bounded height it must grow sideways (infinitely many pairwise incomparable elements).

**Herbrand reduction.** Let T_H be the universal theory of a lattice (∨, ∧ axiomatized as least upper / greatest lower bounds) with no chain of H + 2 elements, carrying monotone F and maps a, b satisfying pins, exclusions, polarity, (semi-)commutation and ⊥ ≠ ⊤. Lattices of finite height are complete, so the Realizability Criterion applies to every model, and intervals do not increase height: **CL holds on all lattices of edge-height ≤ H — infinite ones included — iff T_H is unsatisfiable, iff some finite set of ground instances of T_H over the terms generated from ⊥, ⊤ by F, a, b, ∨, ∧ is unsatisfiable** (Herbrand). Each height has either a finite refutation certificate or a counterexample. Dropping ∨, ∧ gives the poset theory T_H^≤, whose unsatisfiability is a stronger, sufficient condition with a smaller term universe. Status (`theory_z3.py`): T₂^≤ is unsat (z3) — CL on all bounded posets of edge-height ≤ 2, re-proving No-Room for infinite M_κ; controls: without commutation T₂^≤ has a 4-element model; with totality added, T₃^≤ (chains of ≤ 4 elements) is unsat. T₃ (D_ω, M_ω × C₂) and T₄ (E_ω) time out in z3 and Vampire — as does the *chain* control at H = 4, which is a known theorem — so generic quantifier instantiation is the bottleneck, not the mathematics.

**The Ladder, verified symbolically** (`ladder_z3.py`). Over the first-order theory of a bounded chain with λ > ⊥ and a monotone escape s (x < s(x) < λ for x < λ), z3 proves all nine obligations of §22.3 — monotonicity in both arguments, both pins, both exclusions, commutation, polarity, ⊥ ≠ ⊤. Dropping s(x) < λ breaks exactly one obligation, the greatest-fixed-point exclusion: the collision rung of §23.1(iii). The escape axioms have no model with ≤ 5 elements.

## 30.6 Honest scope

- §30.1 decides finite instances only; no SAT instance speaks to E_ω, D_ω or any infinite lattice. "CL holds on all lattices with ≤ 4 join-irreducibles" is a complete finite census, not a theorem about larger lattices.
- The Semi-Commutation Lemma is proved for finite chains; on lattices it is verified, not proved. The Cut Lemma is a conjecture with the evidence stated above and a proof for principal cuts.
- DRAT certificates were checked for the instances marked in §30.1; the other unsat answers are solver verdicts, each with a semantic re-check of every sat answer (none occurred).
- The random cut-lemma sample (50,635 instances) is a sample, not a census; `cutlemma.py` reproduces it with seed 99 given a long enough time budget.

## 30.7 Open problems (after round twenty-two)

1. **The Cut Lemma** on finite lattices — it implies finite semi-CL and hence finite CL. A proof must be global (the cover version is false); the principal-cut proposition and the minimal-counterexample reductions of §30.4 are the starting points. Run in the other direction: extract minimal unsatisfiable cores of the cut instances on B₃, B₄, E₃ and read off which cells carry the contradiction.
2. **Height 3, infinite** (D_ω, M_ω × C₂): find a finite ground refutation of T₃ by a lazy instantiation loop (ground terms to depth d, SAT, add violated instances). Divergence with ever-wider models would display the sideways escape of §30.5.
3. **A sideways escape on E_ω.** A violator invariant under a column *automorphism* is impossible: F(⊥, ⊤) is fixed by every automorphism, hence ⊥ or ⊤, against the Corner Lemma (§16.2: ⊥ ⊏ F(⊥, ⊤) ⊏ ⊤ in every polar configuration). So any counterexample on E_ω must break the symmetry at the bounds — the analogue of the Ladder, whose escape starts at a seam. The natural finite search is over ℕ-indexed columns with a seam at column 0 and F eventually equivariant under the shift *embedding* c ↦ c + 1, encoded with an explicit prefix of columns and a periodic tail. A solution refutes the chain-finite conjecture at height 4.
4. **Chain-finite lattices of unbounded height** (e.g. the horizontal sum of chains of every finite length): outside both the finite and the bounded-height reductions.
5. **Exact characterization.** I2 holds iff a and b have one common fixed point (§14); semi-commutation suffices on finite chains. Is there a one-sided pair condition that is also necessary on finite lattices?
6. **Failure zone** (unchanged since §15.4): retire or restate as a SAT characterization problem.

Literature note (plain): Knaster–Tarski (fixed points as joins of post-fixed / meets of pre-fixed points), Herbrand's theorem for universal theories with equality, CDCL solving with DRAT proof checking (CaDiCaL, drat-trim), and z3's quantifier instantiation; nothing else is used.
