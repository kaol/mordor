# Twenty-Third Exploration Round (v3.4): The Cut Under the Microscope

Round twenty-two reduced the finite Commutation Lemma, in its stronger semi-commutation form, and the chain-finite conjecture to the **Cut Lemma** (§30.4). This round (§31) studies that statement directly. It gives a reachability reformulation that isolates joins as the entire gap (§31.1). It maps the shape of the contradiction: global, often two-dimensional, sometimes needing more than thirty cells (§31.2). It shows the map-level shortcut is closed, since abstract semi-commuting pairs need not admit principal cuts (§31.3). It extends the evidence to every lattice with ≤ 8 elements, Π₄, B₅ and C₃³ (§31.4), and probes the infinite frontier from both sides: a universal cut theory for the logic route (§31.5) and a seam-and-translation search for a sideways escape on D_ω and E_ω (§31.6). No counterexample appears anywhere. The Cut Lemma remains open. Scripts: `experiments/sat/` (files listed in §31.7).

## 31.1 Reachability: the gap is joins

Fix monotone F on a complete lattice L with derived maps a, b. Let **R(F)** be the least down-set that contains ⊥, is closed under a, and contains y whenever it contains b(y).

**Proposition.** The Cut Lemma holds for F iff R(F) = L.

*Proof.* A cut counterexample D is a down-set containing ⊥ with a(D) ⊆ D and b(L ∖ D) ⊆ L ∖ D, i.e. b⁻¹(D) ⊆ D. So R(F) ⊆ D ≠ L. Conversely, if R(F) ≠ L then R(F) is itself a counterexample. ∎

Read as a path problem: ⊤ must be reachable from ⊥ by moves x → a(x), x → y whenever b(y) ≤ x, and free descents. The *ideal* version is settled. Let r̂ be the least element with a(r̂) ≤ r̂ and (b(y) ≤ r̂ ⟹ y ≤ r̂); such elements are closed under meets, so r̂ exists. Then ↓r̂ is a principal cut, so the principal-cut proposition of §30.4 forces r̂ = ⊤ in any lattice without infinite descending chains. Since R(F) ⊆ ↓r̂ with equality exactly when R(F) is join-closed, we get:

> **The Cut Lemma is equivalent to: R(F) is closed under binary joins.**

On a chain every down-set is an ideal, which is why chains were easy (§30.3).

## 31.2 The shape of the contradiction

- **Mostly not one-dimensional** (`cut_onedim.py`). On a chain the single row x = max D is already infeasible. Over all 4,519 (lattice, down-set) instances with ≤ 4 join-irreducibles plus E₃, a single row x ∈ D or column y ∈ U is infeasible on its own in only **2,134** cases. In the rest, rows and columns must be combined.
- **Smallest two-dimensional case**, on {⊥, a, b, c, d = a∨b, ⊤} with D = {⊥, a, b}:
  1. Meets down column d (F(⊥,d) ≤ F(a,d) ∧ F(b,d), with F(⊥,d) ≠ ⊥) force F(a,d) = F(b,d) = c.
  2. Joins along row a then force F(a,⊤) ≥ F(a,c) ∨ c = ⊤, which the cut forbids.

  So column constraints act through meets in D, row constraints through joins in U.
- **Not local** (`cut_mus.py`). Minimal contradictory cell sets range from 1 to **34** cells (distribution recorded in the script output). In 3,307 of 4,451 instances they use rows outside max(D) ∪ {⊥} or columns outside min(U) ∪ {⊤}. Together with §30.4's failure of the cover-only version, this rules out a proof that inspects a bounded neighbourhood of the cut.

## 31.3 The map-level shortcut is closed

The principal-cut proposition needs only maps: a principal ↓d with a(d) ≤ d and (y ≰ d ⟹ b(y) ≰ d), or dually ↑u. So semi-CL would follow if semi-commutation alone forced such a cut. **It does not** (`abstract_pairs.py`). Abstract polar monotone pairs with b∘a ⊑ a∘b and no principal cut of either kind exist already on the six-element lattice above. Listing values in the order (⊥, a, b, a∨b, c, ⊤), one is a = (⊥, ⊥, a, ⊤, ⊤, ⊤), b = (⊥, ⊥, ⊥, c, c, ⊤). That stays true after adding the realizability-implied cross-fix laws F4′ and F5 (§24.2). Any proof has to use F, not just (a, b).

## 31.5 The universal cut theory

The negation of the Cut Lemma is a universal theory C_H in the signature (≤, ∨, ∧, F, D, ⊥, ⊤): a lattice with no chain of H + 2 elements, D a down-set with ⊥ ∈ D and ⊤ ∉ D, F monotone, and F(x, y) ≰ x, F(x, y) ≱ y for x ∈ D, y ∉ D. There are no fixed-point maps, and the Herbrand universe is generated from ⊥, ⊤ by F, ∨, ∧. **Unsatisfiability of C_H proves the Cut Lemma, hence semi-CL and CL, on every lattice of edge-height ≤ H, infinite ones included** (via §30.4).

z3 (`cut_theory_z3.py`): C₁ and C₂ are **unsat**, also in the poset version without ∨, ∧. This is a third proof of CL at edge-height ≤ 2 (after No-Room and T₂^≤ of §30.5). C₃ times out, and so does C₃ with totality, i.e. chains of ≤ 4 elements, where the lemma is a theorem. The instantiation problem, not the mathematics, is again the bottleneck.

One structural remark settles which terms a refutation needs. In the principal-cut proof the witness is a Kleene iterate τᵏ(⊥) with k ≤ H + 1, and the height axiom applied to τ⁰(⊥), …, τ^{H+1}(⊥) closes it. So any lazy instantiation loop should seed its term set with such iterates rather than with depth-bounded closures.

## 31.6 The infinite frontier: no uniform sideways escape

The Escape Lemma (§30.5) says an infinite counterexample of bounded height must grow sideways. The log's ω+1 method (§21.3) looks for its truncations: configurations commuting everywhere except at an artificial seam.

- **Unrestricted seam** (`seam_search.py`). On D_k (columns of 2-chains, edge-height 3) and E_k (columns of 3-chains, edge-height 4), polar realizable pairs that commute everywhere except on the last column exist for every k = 2..7. They are degenerate, though: interior columns are dumped into the seam column (e.g. a sends every interior element of D_k to the top of the last column). One such pattern, a ≡ b ≡ ⊥ on all columns, is not realizable on E_ω by hand. Polarity forces F(⊥, ⊤) into some column. The column-⊤ exclusion then pushes F(x, ⊤) strictly upward along that column, and at the column's top it would have to equal ⊤, which the row exclusion forbids.
- **Uniform seam** (`seam_equivariant.py`). Require F, a, b to commute with the column shift i ↦ i + 1 on cells away from the seam, with locality: no interior value may jump into the seam columns. Cells with both coordinates in {⊥, ⊤} are exempt, since the Corner Lemma forbids their invariance (§30.7). Result: **unsat for every k = 4..8, on D_k and on E_k, with seams of one and of two columns.** Satisfiable instances occur only at k = 3, where the interior is too small for the constraints to bind.

So there is no translation-invariant, bounded-range sideways escape on D_ω or E_ω with escape range ≤ 2 columns. The most natural infinite construction, the column analogue of the Ladder, is excluded. This is evidence for CL on D_ω and E_ω, not a proof: a counterexample could still be non-uniform or have unbounded range.

## 31.7 Honest scope, scripts, open problems

- Everything here is evidence or reformulation; no new class of lattices is proved. The Cut Lemma, and with it finite CL beyond the lattices decided by SAT, is open.
- The B₅ and C₃³ checks are partial (sampled down-set classes); Π₄ and the ≤ 8-element family are complete.
- Scripts: `cut_onedim.py`, `cut_mus.py`, `abstract_pairs.py`, `small_lattices.py`, `hunt.py`, `cut_theory_z3.py`, `seam_search.py`, `seam_equivariant.py`.

**Open problems (after round twenty-three).**

1. **Join-closure of R(F)** (§31.1): equivalent to the Cut Lemma. The two-dimensional pattern of §31.2 (meets down columns of D, joins along rows of U) is the mechanism a proof has to capture.
2. **Lazy instantiation for C₃**, seeded with Kleene iterates (§31.5). Unsat proves CL on every lattice of edge-height ≤ 3, including D_ω and M_ω × C₂.
3. **Non-uniform sideways escapes** on D_ω and E_ω: drop equivariance but keep locality, or allow escape ranges growing with k (§31.6).
4. Carried over: exact characterization of μν = νμ by a one-sided pair condition (§30.7(5)); chain-finite lattices of unbounded height; the failure zone.
