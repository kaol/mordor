# Twenty-Fourth Exploration Round (v3.5): The Dialgebraic Layer, Read Thin

The dialgebraic axioms D1–D5 (§2) have so far met the research program only as a census of the Lambek property (§12.2). Two negative results followed: no preservation invariant predicts δ₀ iso-ness (§13.3), and neither does an exchange law (§15.3). This round reads the layer in its thin model, a complete lattice as a poset category, and connects it to the rest of the framework.

- **Transposition (§32.2).** When G is a right adjoint, an (F, G)-dialgebra is a (G*∘F)-algebra. So D1 holds there, and dually D2 holds when F is a left adjoint.
- **Failure zone (§32.3).** The §13.3 negative is overturned. For meet-preserving G, δ₀ is iso iff F(A₀) ∈ Im G: the zone is the unit of G* ⊣ G. Dually, for join-preserving F, δ₁ is iso iff G(A₁) ∈ Im F. Both are exact over every pair in five censuses.
- **D5 (§32.4).** Through an anti-automorphism, D5 is the engine of the Duality Theorem (§22.1). It also explains the 1,688 M3 pairs of §15.3.
- **The CL program (§32.5).** Its objects are slice dialgebras. Naive dialgebras of the derived pair do not encode μν = νμ.
- **The Cut Lemma (§32.6).** It is a heterogeneous dialgebra L → 𝒪(L). Transposed along sup ⊣ ↓, it becomes the principal-cut proposition, which is proved. The comparison square is lax. **New:** the lax square is tight along the actual orbit, for every F, on every lattice in which L ∖ {⊤} is a tree. On every other lattice checked it fails, already for some F.

Scripts are in `experiments/dialgebra/` (§32.8). The GPU census (`gpu/`) now measures orbit laxity (§32.6.4).

## 32.1 The thin reading

Let L be a complete lattice viewed as a category: one arrow x → y iff x ≤ y. Endofunctors are monotone maps, and natural transformations are pointwise inequalities.

- An **(f, g)-dialgebra** is an x with f(x) ≤ g(x). Let Dial(f, g) = {x : f(x) ≤ g(x)}.
- Every arrow between dialgebras is a dialgebra morphism, because the square commutes in a thin category.
- D1 asks for the least element A₀ of Dial(f, g), and δ₀ is iso iff f(A₀) = g(A₀).
- D2 asks for the greatest element A₁, and δ₁ is iso iff f(A₁) = g(A₁).
- Dial(f, g) is in general closed under neither meets nor joins. It can be empty (≈ 10 % of chain pairs, §12.2) or have incomparable minima (the strict M3 count, §15.3). The axioms are not theorems here, and the question is *when* they hold.

## 32.2 Transposition: when D1 and D2 hold

**Theorem (transposition).** Let g preserve all meets, so that it has a left adjoint g* (g*(x) = ⋀{y : x ≤ g(y)}). Then f(x) ≤ g(x) ⟺ g*(f(x)) ≤ x. So Dial(f, g) is the set of prefixed points of the monotone map g*∘f, and by Knaster–Tarski
```
A₀ = μ^{f,g} = μ(g*∘f).
```
Dually, if f preserves all joins with right adjoint f_*, then Dial(f, g) is the set of postfixed points of f_*∘g, and A₁ = ν^{f,g} = ν(f_*∘g). ∎

- Bd9 is the case g = Id (respectively f = Id).
- In a finite lattice, binary meets alone already make Dial(f, g) meet-closed: f(x ∧ y) ≤ f(x) ∧ f(y) ≤ g(x) ∧ g(y) = g(x ∧ y). So a least dialgebra exists whenever Dial(f, g) ≠ ∅.
- The adjoint form is checked by `lambek_zone.py` over every pair with g meet-preserving and g(⊤) = ⊤: C3 60/60, C4 700/700, M3 8,900/8,900, N5 5,848/5,848, B2 576/576.

## 32.3 The failure-zone theorem

**Theorem.** Let g preserve binary meets and let A₀ be the least (f, g)-dialgebra. Then
```
δ₀ is iso  ⟺  f(A₀) ∈ Im g.
```
*Proof.*
1. (⟹) f(A₀) = g(A₀).
2. (⟸) Let f(A₀) = g(z) and put x = A₀ ∧ z.
3. Then f(x) ≤ f(A₀) = g(z) and f(x) ≤ f(A₀) ≤ g(A₀), so f(x) ≤ g(A₀) ∧ g(z) = g(x). So x is a dialgebra.
4. Since x ≤ A₀ and A₀ is least, x = A₀, so A₀ ≤ z.
5. Hence g(A₀) ≤ g(z) = f(A₀) ≤ g(A₀). ∎

**Reading through §32.2.** When g has a left adjoint, A₀ = g*f(A₀) is a fixed point of g*∘f: the transposed algebra always satisfies Lambek. The dialgebra map δ₀ is the unit η of g* ⊣ g at f(A₀), followed by the identity:
```
f(A₀) ≤ g g* f(A₀) = g(A₀).
```
So δ₀ is iso iff f(A₀) is g g*-closed, i.e. iff f(A₀) ∈ Im g. **The failure zone of dialgebraic Lambek is exactly the failure of the unit of the adjunction to be iso at the point f(A₀).**

Both positive classes of the log are special cases:
- the (F, Id) Lambek slice, where Im Id = L;
- the doubly-subidentity class of §15.2, where A₀ = ⊥ = f(⊥) = g(⊥) in a poset.

So is every surjective meet-preserving g.

**Dual theorem.** Let f preserve binary joins and let A₁ be the greatest dialgebra. Then δ₁ is iso ⟺ g(A₁) ∈ Im f.

It is false for invariants of the *pair and its initial object*: the criterion f(A₀) ∈ Im g is exact for meet-preserving g and correct on 99.3–99.7 % of all pairs that have an initial dialgebra. "Tightness, not preservation" (§13.3) was the right instinct: Im g is precisely the set where the transposed tightness can be reflected back.

## 32.4 D5 is the Duality Theorem

Let ρ : L ≅ Lᵒᵖ be an anti-automorphism. Then
```
x ∈ Dial(f, g)  ⟺  ρx ∈ Dial(ρgρ, ρfρ),
```
because f(x) ≤ g(x) ⟺ ρg(x) ≤ ρf(x) and ρρ = id. Since ρ reverses order, least maps to greatest and δ₀ iso maps to δ₁ iso. This is D5, "μ^{FG} on M = ν^{GF} on Mᵒᵖ", with the swap of the two functors made visible. `duality.py` checks it on every pair: C4 1,225/1,225, M3 31,684/31,684, N5 18,496/18,496, B2 1,296/1,296.

**The 1,688 M3 pairs (§15.3) explained.** D5 does not pair initial(f, g) with final(f, g). It pairs initial(f, g) with final(ρgρ, ρfρ). The aggregate equality of initial and final counts (29,999 = 29,999, iso 25,073 = 25,073) holds because (f, g) ↦ (ρgρ, ρfρ) is an involution on Mon(L)² whenever L is self-dual, and C_n, M3, N5 and B2 all are. Per-pair coincidence at the *same* pair has no reason to hold, and fails 1,688 times on M3.

**The Duality Theorem (§22.1) is D5 applied fibrewise.**
- a(x) = νY.F(x, Y) is the final (Id, F(x, –))-dialgebra.
- b(y) = μX.F(X, y) is the initial (F(–, y), Id)-dialgebra.
- D5 with h = ρ sends the initial (F(–, y), Id)-dialgebra to the final (Id, ρF(–, y)ρ)-dialgebra, i.e. to row ρy of Fᵈ(x, y) = h(F(h(y), h(x))). That is aᵈ = h∘b∘h⁻¹.

The "outer reversal" that a first implementation missed in §22.1 is the conjugation by ρ that D5 demands. The transpose alone swaps rows and columns, but it does not swap initial and final.

## 32.5 The CL program in dialgebraic terms

- **Objects.** The derived pair consists of slice dialgebras:
  - a(x) is the final (Id, F_x)-dialgebra and b(y) the initial (F^y, Id)-dialgebra. Both are Bd9 reductions, so D1/D2 hold.
  - m = μa is the initial (a, Id)-dialgebra and n = νb the final (Id, b)-dialgebra.
  - The Realizability Criterion (§24.1) says what it takes for given maps (a, b) to arise this way. Pins make each slice dialgebra a Lambek fixed point (δ iso). Exclusions say there is no larger (respectively smaller) slice dialgebra.
- **Bounds.** I1 (m ≤ n) compares an initial dialgebra of one slice family with a final dialgebra of the other. It is *not* the cross bound Bd8, which compares μ^{F,G} with ν^{G,F} for the same pair. It needs the bifunction, through L1 a(n) = n and L2 b(m) = m (§13). I2 (m = n ⟺ a unique common fixed point) is a tightness statement: the bi-Lambek set Fix(a) ∩ Fix(b) is a singleton.
- **Negative: naive dialgebras of the derived pair do not encode m = n** (`naive_ab.py`). For every F (exhaustive on C4 and B2, 20,000 heat-bath samples on N5, M3 and B3), the test was whether the least (a, b)- or (b, a)-dialgebra is Lambek-iso, compared against m = n. Agreement is below that of the constant predictor (always guessing the majority value of m = n) on every lattice:

  | Lattice | iso₀(a, b) agrees with m = n | iso₀(b, a) agrees with m = n | constant predictor |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | C4 | 69.8 % | 36.9 % | 70.5 % |
  | B2 | 68.1 % | 34.7 % | 71.0 % |
  | N5 | 52.0 % | 27.4 % | 54.5 % |
  | M3 | 50.6 % | 25.5 % | 51.7 % |
  | B3 | 24.1 % | 13.6 % | 73.0 % |

  The least (a, b)-dialgebra equals m in only 79 % of C4 cases. CL is a statement about F, not about (a, b): compare §31.3, where the map-level shortcut also failed.

## 32.6 The Cut Lemma as a dialgebra L → 𝒪(L)

### 32.6.1 Principal cuts transpose to a κ-algebra

Let 𝒪(L) be the lattice of down-sets ordered by inclusion, with ↓ : L → 𝒪(L) the principal embedding and sup ⊣ ↓. A principal counterexample ↓d to the Cut Lemma (§31.3) is a d with a(d) ≤ d and (b(y) ≤ d ⟹ y ≤ d), i.e. an (F′, ↓)-dialgebra between two categories (Hagino's form; D1–D5 are stated for endofunctors, and this is their heterogeneous extension):
```
F′(d) = ↓a(d) ∪ b⁻¹(↓d)  ⊆  ↓d.
```
Transposing along sup ⊣ ↓ (sup D ≤ d ⟺ D ⊆ ↓d) gives F′(d) ⊆ ↓d ⟺ κ(d) ≤ d, where
```
κ(d) = sup F′(d) = a(d) ∨ ⋁{y : b(y) ≤ d}.
```
- Principal cuts are the prefixed points of κ, the least one is μκ = r̂ (round 23's ideal closure, §31.1), and the principal-cut proposition (§30.4) is **μκ = ⊤**.
- `cut_transpose.py` confirms the transposition on every (F, d) cell: N5 20,000, B3 32,000, E2 32,000, M3 20,000. It also finds μκ = ⊤ and R(F) = L on all 4 × 4,000 sampled F.

### 32.6.2 The full lemma and the lax square

The full Cut Lemma is about the initial algebra of
```
Θ(D) = ↓⊥ ∪ ↓a(D) ∪ b⁻¹(D)
```
on 𝒪(L), with μΘ = R(F), and the Cut Lemma ⟺ μΘ = L. For s = sup D we have a(D) ⊆ ↓a(s) and b⁻¹(D) ⊆ {y : b(y) ≤ s}, so
```
sup ∘ Θ  ≤  κ ∘ sup        (a lax square; sup is only an oplax morphism Θ → κ).
```
If the square commuted, sup would be a strict algebra morphism, and μ-fusion would give sup μΘ = μκ = ⊤. Being lax, it only gives sup μΘ ≤ μκ, which is the useless direction.

This is the I11 pattern: the comparison 2-cell between the two layers (down-set closure and ideal closure) is not invertible, so the layers are strictly nested. The Cut Lemma asks whether they nevertheless reach the same top. Even sup R(F) = ⊤ would not suffice, because the lemma needs ⊤ ∈ R(F), i.e. membership.

On arbitrary down-sets the square is strict sometimes: `cut_transpose.py` finds such D for N5 in 46/4,000 sampled F, for B3 in 2, for E2 in 3 and for M3 in 0. The decisive question is the orbit.

### 32.6.3 The orbit: tight exactly on trees

Run both closures in step, inflationary:
```
R₀ = ↓⊥,  R_{k+1} = ↓(R_k ∪ a(R_k) ∪ b⁻¹(R_k));    r₀ = ⊥,  r_{k+1} = r_k ∨ κ(r_k).
```
The lax square gives ⋁R_k ≤ r_k by induction. Call F **orbit-tight** if ⋁R_k = r_k for all k.

**Lemma (principal steps are tight).** If R_k = ↓s and r_k = s, then ⋁R_{k+1} = r_{k+1}.

*Proof.* R_{k+1} = ↓s ∪ ↓a(s) ∪ {y : b(y) ≤ s}, whose join is s ∨ a(s) ∨ ⋁{y : b(y) ≤ s} = s ∨ κ(s). ∎

So strictness can first appear only right after a *non-principal* R_k with ⋁R_k = r_k < ⊤. Call L **top-closed** if every non-principal down-set has join ⊤. Equivalently:
- every two incomparable elements join to ⊤;
- ↓x is a chain for every x < ⊤;
- L ∖ {⊤} is a rooted tree, with the root at ⊥ and branching upward.

**Theorem.** On a top-closed lattice every F is orbit-tight.

*Proof.* By induction on k, using the Lemma while R_k is principal. If R_k is non-principal, then ⋁R_k = ⊤ = r_k, and both sequences stay at ⊤ from then on. ∎

**Conjecture (converse).** If L is not top-closed, some F on L is not orbit-tight.

Evidence (`orbit_lax.py`). z3 decides existence of F with ⋁R_k ≠ r_k for some k, and every witness is re-checked by `gpu/reference.py` and by a plain-set recomputation.
- **Every lattice of size 5, 6 and 7** (5 + 15 + 53 = 73 lattices). The tallies are 4, 9 and 20 top-closed, all tight, matching the rooted-tree counts with 4, 5 and 6 nodes (OEIS A000081). The 1, 6 and 33 others all have witnesses.
- C4, B2, M3, N5, E2 and D3 are top-closed and tight (unsat). C2×C3, B3 and C3×C3 are not top-closed and have witnesses.

A polar witness on B3, with elements ⊥ = 0 < atoms 1, 2, 4 < 3, 5, 6 < 7 and joins by bitwise or: a = (0, 5, 7, 7, 0, 7, 7, 7), b = (0, 0, 5, 5, 0, 4, 5, 7). The orbit gives R₁ = ↓{1, 4} with join 5 = r₁. At the next step a(5) = 7 forces r₂ = ⊤, while a(1) = 5, a(4) = 0 and b⁻¹(R₁) ⊆ ↓5 keep ⋁R₂ = 5. The lag is a(sup) ≠ sup a(…), i.e. a failing to preserve the join 1 ∨ 4.

**Exact law (CPU census of every F).** The two dual 5-element lattices differ:

| Lattice | F | orbit-strict F | of which polar | first strict stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ⊕ B2 (L5_2, top-closed) | 22,969,892 | 0 | 0 of 10,937,506 | — |
| B2 ⊕ 1 (L5_3) | 22,969,892 | 1,360,132 (5.92 %) | 721,934 of 10,937,506 (6.60 %) | 2, always |

Orbit tightness is therefore not self-dual, though the Cut Lemma is (by §22.1). The transposition chose sup ⊣ ↓, and its dual, through ↑ and inf, is tight on the dual lattices.

The C4, B2, N5 and M3 censuses (24,696 / 28,224 / 31,022,611 / 129,157,925 F) have 0 strict, as the Theorem demands.

**Delay.** Does R_{k+d} always catch up with r_k, i.e. is there a delay-d simulation of the κ-orbit by the Θ-orbit? Let d(L) be the least d with r_k ≤ ⋁R_{k+d} for all F and k.
- d(L) = 0 iff L is top-closed (Theorem and evidence above).
- d(L) = 1 on C2×C3, B3 and the single non-top-closed 5-element lattice.
- On C3×C3, d = 2: z3 finds an F with (⋁R_k, r_k) = (⊥, ⊥), (4, 4), (7, ⊤), (7, ⊤), (⊤, ⊤), where 4 is the centre and 7 a coatom; the delay-2 query is unsat.
- Across all 6-element lattices: d = 0 on 9, d = 1 on 5, and d ≥ 2 on 1.
- Across all 7-element lattices: d = 0 on 20, d = 1 on 20, and d ≥ 2 on 13. Each lattice in the last group carries a z3 witness of delay exactly 2, and higher delays were not searched there.

Off trees, then, the delay is not 1 in general.

A bounded-delay simulation would prove ⋁R(F) = ⊤ (which already follows from tightness on top-closed L), never membership. The membership gap is what remains of the Cut Lemma in either case.

**What this says about the Cut Lemma.**
- On a top-closed lattice, ⋁R(F) = μκ = ⊤ for every F. A counterexample R(F) ≠ L would have to be a closed, non-principal down-set with join ⊤, i.e. a union of at least two branches of the tree avoiding ⊤. The gap there is pure membership.
- Off trees, the ideal orbit runs ahead of the down-set orbit. Any proof that compares the two orbits stage by stage must allow lag, and the lag grows with the lattice (d = 2 already on C3×C3).

## 32.7 Consequences for the framework

1. **D1 and D2 get sufficient conditions.** D1 holds when G is a right adjoint, with μ^{F,G} = μ(G*∘F) and dfold the fold of G*∘F transposed. D2 holds when F is a left adjoint. Bd9 becomes a corollary (G = Id, F = Id).
2. **The failure zone is closed for meet-preserving G** (dually, join-preserving F): it is the non-invertibility of the unit at F(A₀). What remains open is the zone for G without a left adjoint, which is where all 241 census mismatches lie.
3. **D5 is used, not just checked.** It is the Duality Theorem (§22.1), and I10's μ → ν conversion is transport along ρ, valid outright when L is self-dual. In the thin model I9 degenerates, because dfold and dunfold are inequalities.
4. **The CL program uses the layer through slice dialgebras only.** The pair (a, b) does not carry CL as a dialgebra property (§32.5), consistent with §31.3.
5. **The Cut Lemma is the first genuinely heterogeneous dialgebra in the log.** Its transposition along sup ⊣ ↓ isolates the proved part (μκ = ⊤). Its laxity is an instance of I11's strictly nested layers, and the laxity is located exactly: it is absent on trees, present elsewhere, and its delay grows.
