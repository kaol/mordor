# Twenty-Fifth Exploration Round (v3.6): The First GPU Census

The `gpu/` engine (rounds 22–24) was run on a Radeon Pro W6600: **373,086,957,077 bifunctions enumerated exactly** on 13 distinct lattices of 3–6 elements, and **241,254,400 heat-bath samples** on 20 lattices of up to 32 elements. Every tripwire is 0 and every conjecture counter is 0 (§33.1).

The orbit-tightness law of §32.6.3 is confirmed on the measured laws of all 15 six-element lattices (§33.2). The data led to a new theorem: **the Cut Lemma is self-dual for each F individually**, because the down-set and up-set closures are adjoint under complement (§33.3). The uniform law on F concentrates on two generic classes and gives vanishing mass to the configurations the CL program studies (§33.4). That fixes what the next GPU run has to measure (§33.5).

Raw data: `data/gpu-2026-10/` (results.jsonl, batch.log). Tables: `gpu/analyze.py`. Script: `experiments/dialgebra/cut_adjoint.py`.

## 33.3 Theorem: the Cut Lemma is self-dual per F

For monotone a, b on a finite lattice L, put
```
Φ(D) = ↓(D ∪ a(D) ∪ b⁻¹(D))   on down-sets,      R_k = Φᵏ(↓⊥),
Ψ(U) = ↑(U ∪ b(U) ∪ a⁻¹(U))   on up-sets,        U_j = Ψʲ(↑⊤).
```
R(F) = ⋃R_k is the closure of §31.1. U(F) = ⋃U_j is the closure the Cut Lemma's up-set form uses (the image of R under §22.1's duality).

**Lemma (adjunction).** For a down-set D and an up-set U: Φ(D) ∩ U = ∅ ⟺ D ∩ Ψ(U) = ∅.

*Proof.* Both sides say the same three things: D ∩ U = ∅; a(D) ∩ U = ∅, i.e. no x ∈ D has a(x) ∈ U (the ↓ is harmless because U is an up-set); and b(U) ∩ D = ∅, i.e. no y ∈ U has b(y) ∈ D (the ↑ is harmless because D is a down-set). ∎

With c the complement 𝒪(L) ≅ Up(L)ᵒᵖ, this says **Φ ⊣ c∘Ψ∘c**. Φ preserves unions, and its right adjoint is the up-set closure step seen through complements.

**Theorem.** For all k, j ≥ 0:
```
R_k ∩ U_j = ∅  ⟺  R_{k+j} ≠ L  ⟺  U_{k+j} ≠ L.
```
*Proof.* Apply the Lemma k times to get R_k ∩ U_j = ∅ ⟺ ↓⊥ ∩ U_{k+j} = ∅ ⟺ ⊥ ∉ U_{k+j}. An up-set contains ⊥ iff it is L. Applying it j times the other way gives R_{k+j} ∩ ↑⊤ = ∅ ⟺ R_{k+j} ≠ L. ∎

**Corollaries.**
1. **R(F) = L ⟺ U(F) = L.** The Cut Lemma for F in down-set form and in up-set form are the same statement. A cut is a Φ-algebra D whose complement is automatically a Ψ-algebra (Φ(D) ⊆ D ⟺ D ⊆ cΨc(D)), so the two halves of the definition, a(D) ⊆ D and b(U) ⊆ U, are one condition under the adjunction.
2. **The closures need the same number of stages, F by F.** This explains the identical `rstages` histograms of L5_2/L5_3 and L6_9/L6_13. With §22.1's duality F ↦ Fᵈ, the down-set stages of Fᵈ on Lᵒᵖ are the up-set stages of F on L, hence the down-set stages of F.
3. **The distance profiles are complementary.** If N(F) is the stage count, then R_k and U_j meet iff k + j ≥ N(F). The down-set closure and the up-set closure are two halves of a single path of length N from ⊥ to ⊤.
4. **Only monotonicity is used.** Realizability is never needed: `cut_adjoint.py` checks the identity over all of Mon(L)², 1,225 to 31,684 pairs per lattice, on every lattice of size 4 and 5.

**Contrast with §32.6.** The κ-orbit is *not* dual-invariant (L6_9 is tight, L6_13 lags on 6.23 % of F). The adjunction Φ ⊣ cΨc lives in 𝒪(L). The transposition along sup ⊣ ↓ that produces κ picks one side of L, and its dual uses ↑ ⊣ inf. So the symmetry is lost exactly where the proof strategy "compare with the ideal orbit" lives. A proof of the Cut Lemma that respects Corollary 1 should stay in 𝒪(L) or use both transpositions at once.

## 33.4 The shape of the uniform law: two generic classes

From `gpu/analyze.py` (percent of all F; census exact, mcmc estimates):

| Lattice | n | commuting | m = n | polar | m ≠ n, not polar | m = n, not commuting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C4 | 4 | 50.41 | 70.52 | 26.08 | 3.40 | 20.11 |
| C6 | 6 | 16.52 | 33.29 | 59.79 | 6.92 | 16.77 |
| L6_13 | 6 | 17.93 | 34.34 | 62.69 | 2.97 | 16.41 |
| B3 | 8 | 25.00 | 27.08 | 72.88 | 0.038 | 2.08 |
| D4 | 10 | 21.80 | 22.02 | 77.98 | 0.0001 | 0.22 |
| E4 | 14 | 15.66 | 15.71 | 84.29 | 0 | 0.045 |
| B4 | 16 | 12.49 | 12.52 | 87.48 | 0 | 0.026 |
| Π₄ | 15 | 13.52 | 13.52 | 86.48 | 0 | 0.0003 |
| C4×C4 | 16 | 1.76 | 1.81 | 98.19 | 0 | 0.046 |
| B5 | 32 | 6.2392 | 6.2393 | 93.7607 | 0 | 0.00005 |

As L grows, almost every F falls into one of two classes:
- **(G1) commuting with m = n**: one common fixed point, as I2 requires;
- **(G2) polar** (which forces m = ⊥ ≠ ⊤ = n), with the profile measured on B4, E4, Π₄ and B5:
  - Fix(a) ∩ Fix(b) = {⊥, ⊤}: ncf = 2 on exactly the polar samples;
  - a∘b ⊑ b∘a, failing on only 1 of 11,466,348 polar samples on B4 and 18 of 11,048,152 on E4;
  - κ(⊥) = ⊤ in one step;
  - R₁ = ↓b⁻¹(⊥) non-principal, and R₂ = L.

The mechanism is the bottom row. b⁻¹(⊥) = {y : F(⊥, y) = ⊥}, and under the uniform law that set is large with join ⊤. So the principal step κ(⊥) = ⋁b⁻¹(⊥) already reaches ⊤, and the Cut Lemma instance is trivial.

What the CL program studies lies in the vanishing complement:
- commuting polar pairs, the shape of a CL counterexample;
- semi-commuting polar pairs;
- Cut instances that need more than the principal step;
- orbit lag.

Uniform sampling on large lattices is therefore a **calibration instrument, not a search**: 0 counts there carry little evidential weight. The SAT decisions of rounds 22–23 remain the evidence of record beyond six elements.

Smaller exact facts from the census:
- **Polar implies a∘b ⊑ b∘a** holds exactly on C3 (20/20) and C4 (6,440/6,440). It fails on B2 (392 F), C5 (68,920 F) and C6 (660,824,760 F, 1.20 %). This dual one-sided relation is near-universal but not a law.
- **On height-2 lattices** (B2, M3, M4), m ≠ n holds exactly for the polar F: the column "m ≠ n, not polar" is 0. Reason: no F on C2 is polar, so m ≠ n forces |[m, n]| ≥ 3, i.e. [m, n] = L.

## 33.5 Consequences and next runs

1. **Condition the sampler.** The heat bath stays exact when every cell is confined to an interval [ℓ_c, u_c]. Monotone F inside a box form a distributive lattice, and the kernel stays reversible and irreducible. Boxes reach the hard core:
   - F(⊥, ⊥) = ⊥ and F(⊤, ⊤) = ⊤, for the polar corners;
   - lower bounds F(⊥, y) ≥ ℓ(y) on the bottom row, which keep b⁻¹(⊥) small and force κ(⊥) < ⊤.

   Exact reweighting by box volume (Knuth estimates restricted to the box) turns conditional rates back into unconditional ones. Tempering on a score (κ-steps, R-stages) is the alternative.
2. **Exact orbit-lag laws** for the remaining non-top-closed six-element lattices. L6_12 (est. 1.4·10¹¹) and L6_11 (2.3·10¹¹) are within reach of the census; L6_4 and L6_10 (≈ 6·10¹¹ and 10¹²) are larger. Raise the Knuth paths to 65,536 before trusting `auto` near its threshold.
3. **Theory.** Corollary 1 says a cut is a single adjunction-closed object. Restate the join-closure problem (§31.1) in 𝒪(L) with Φ ⊣ cΨc, and ask whether the two-dimensional pattern of §31.2 (meets down columns of D, joins along rows of U) is the unit and counit of this adjunction.
